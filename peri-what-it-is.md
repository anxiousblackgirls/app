Perimenopause is the stretch of years before your periods stop for good. Hormones rise and fall unevenly, and your body and mood can feel unfamiliar. It often starts in your 40s, sometimes in your late 30s, and menopause itself is official once you've gone 12 months without a period.

## What it can feel like

- Periods that come closer together, farther apart, heavier, or lighter
- Hot flashes and night sweats
- Waking at 3 a.m. and not falling back asleep
- Brain fog and forgetfulness
- Irritability, anxiety, or low mood that feels new
- Joint aches, vaginal dryness, and changes in sex drive

> The Study of Women's Health Across the Nation (SWAN), which has followed thousands of midlife women of different races for decades, found that Black women reported hot flashes and night sweats more often than any other group. A later SWAN analysis found those symptoms lasted longest for Black women, a median of about 10 years. Many of us are told it's "just stress" or "just getting older" and sent home.

## Why it matters for your mental health

Hormone shifts affect the brain chemicals that steady mood, sleep, and focus. If you already live with anxiety, depression, or ADHD, perimenopause can make it louder. If you never had those struggles before, new symptoms in your 40s deserve a real conversation with a provider, not a shrug.

## Try this

- **Start a symptom log today.** Each day, note your period, hot flashes, sleep, and mood from 1 to 10. Two or three months of notes tell a provider far more than "I don't feel like myself."
- **Name it at your next visit.** Say, "I think I'm in perimenopause. Can we talk about my symptoms and treatment options?"
- **Find a menopause-trained clinician.** The Menopause Society lists certified practitioners by zip code at menopause.org.
