Losing a job is hard for anyone. When your kids depend on your paycheck, it can feel like the floor dropped out. Over the past year and a half, many Black women have been living this.

## What happened

In 2025, Black women were hit hard by layoffs. The Institute for Women's Policy Research found that between January and August 2025, Black women lost about 251,000 jobs, more than half of all job losses among women, even though they made up about 14 percent of women in the workforce. Black women also lost more than 30 percent of their federal government jobs that year. By March 2026, Black women's unemployment rate was 6.1 percent, compared with 4.3 percent overall.

> Many Black women took federal and corporate jobs because they promised stability, and many of us are the main earners in our homes. So a layoff can bring more than money stress. It can shake your sense of who you are, especially if you were raised to believe hard work would protect you. You may feel shame, anger, or fear, and still have to cook dinner and help with homework like nothing happened. Those feelings make sense. A layoff driven by budget cuts or policy changes says nothing about your worth.

## How job loss affects your mind

In a study of Black single mothers, not having enough money was the most common reason given for feeling depressed. Financial strain can disrupt sleep, raise anxiety, and make you short-tempered with the people you love most.

## Try this

- **Take care of the basics first.** Apply for unemployment right away, look into health coverage options, and call creditors early to ask about hardship plans.
- **Tell your kids the truth, simply.** "I lost my job. We're going to be okay, and we're making a plan." Kids sense stress, and silence can scare them more.
- **Keep a routine.** Get up, get dressed, and give your days structure, even if you're not going to an office.
- **Lean on your network.** Many laid-off Black women are finding new roles and support through each other. Reach out to former coworkers, alumni groups, and professional organizations.
- **Watch your mood.** If you've felt hopeless or stopped caring about things you love for two weeks or more, talk to a therapist. Some offer sliding-scale fees.
- **Limit doom scrolling.** Check job boards and the news at set times, then put the phone down.
