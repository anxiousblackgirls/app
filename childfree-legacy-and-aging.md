"Who's going to take care of you when you're old?" It's one of the most common questions childfree women hear, and it can stick with you. It's worth an honest look, without fear running the show.

## What childfree adults actually worry about

A 2024 Pew Research Center survey asked adults 50 and older without children about their concerns as they age. Their top worries were having enough money, having someone to care for them, and loneliness. These are real concerns. They're also concerns many parents share, because having children doesn't guarantee they'll be nearby or able to help.

> Black communities have always known that family isn't only made by birth. Patricia Hill Collins described "othermothers," women who helped raise children who weren't biologically theirs, and whose care held whole communities together. Many childfree Black women are aunties, godmothers, mentors, coaches, and church mothers. That is legacy. The people you pour into, the work you do, and the love you give all carry on after you.

## Rethinking legacy

Legacy can mean the students you taught, the business you built, the nieces who call you first, or the family history you preserve. It can mean money you leave to a cause, or traditions you pass on to the next generation of your chosen family. You get to decide what yours looks like.

## Try this

- **Plan ahead on paper.** Make a will, name a health care proxy, and set up a power of attorney. These protect you no matter your family situation.
- **Save for later care.** Talk to a financial planner about retirement and long-term care.
- **Build your circle across ages.** Friendships with people younger and older than you create support that lasts.
- **Invest in the young people around you.** Mentoring, sponsoring, or being the auntie who shows up builds relationships that grow both ways.
- **Write down what legacy means to you.** List the people, causes, and values you want to leave a mark on.
- **Notice when the question hurts.** If "who will take care of you" stirs up fear or loneliness, talk it through with someone you trust or a therapist.
