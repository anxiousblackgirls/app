You hear your mother's voice come out of your mouth. You promised yourself you'd never yell like that, and then you do. Breaking generational patterns is hard, and slipping back into them doesn't mean you've failed.

## What researchers are seeing

In a 2025 study, researchers interviewed 31 Black mothers about the discipline they grew up with and how they parent now. Most had experienced punishment such as spanking or yelling as kids. About two out of three said they had shifted away from those methods with their own children. Some changed after learning about child development, and some changed because of how the old ways made them feel.

The study also found something hopeful about mothers and daughters. Many women felt closer to their own mothers after becoming parents, because they finally understood the pressure their mothers were under. Some relationships were still strained, and that was real too.

> Patterns like harsh discipline, silence about feelings, and "what happens in this house stays in this house" often started as ways to survive. Black parents disciplined hard partly because they feared what the world would do to children who stepped out of line. Knowing that can help you understand your family without excusing the hurt. Breaking a cycle doesn't require calling your parents villains. It means you get to decide what continues.

## Try this

- **Learn your triggers.** Notice when you're most likely to snap: when you're tired, rushed, or feeling disrespected. Plan for those moments.
- **Pause before you respond.** Step away, take a breath, and come back. "I need a minute" is a skill you're teaching your child too.
- **Repair after a rough moment.** "I yelled, and that wasn't okay. I'm sorry. I love you." Repair teaches kids that relationships can recover.
- **Swap punishment for connection when you can.** Ask what's going on before deciding on a consequence.
- **Set boundaries with family advice.** "I know that worked for you. This is what works for us."
- **Heal your own stuff.** Therapy can help you work through what happened to you, so it shows up less in how you parent.
