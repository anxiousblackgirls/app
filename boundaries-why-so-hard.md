A boundary is a limit you set to protect your time, energy, body, or peace. It sounds simple. For many Black women, saying no feels selfish, rude, or even dangerous.

## Why it's hard

> Research on the Strong Black Woman role has found that many Black women feel obligated to help others and to hide their own needs. A study of Black women found that self-silencing, holding back your feelings and needs to keep the peace, was linked to more symptoms of depression. Another study found that the obligation to help others was one of the parts of the Superwoman role most linked to physical wear from stress.

You may have learned that:

- Good daughters, mothers, and church members never say no
- Asking for what you need is selfish
- If you don't do it, nobody will
- Speaking up will get you labeled angry or difficult

## What boundaries are and aren't

A boundary is about what **you** will do, not about controlling someone else. "I'm not able to lend money right now" is a boundary. "You need to stop asking me for money" is a request. Both are okay, but only the first is fully in your control.

## Boundaries protect relationships

When you say yes to everything, resentment builds. Boundaries let you show up for people with love instead of exhaustion.

## Try this

- **Notice your resentment.** Where do you feel bitter or used? That's often where a boundary is missing.
- **Practice a short no.** "I can't this time." No explanation needed. Say it out loud a few times so it feels familiar.
- **Buy yourself time.** "Let me check and get back to you" gives you space to decide what you actually want.
- **Expect discomfort.** Guilt after setting a boundary doesn't mean you did something wrong. It means you're doing something new.
