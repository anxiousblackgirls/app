Stress doesn't only live in your mind. Over years, it shows up in your blood vessels and your heart. For Black women, everyday experiences of unfair treatment are part of that load.

## What research shows

> In a study of Black women in midlife, those who reported more chronic everyday discrimination, like being treated with less respect or as if they weren't smart, had more calcium buildup in the arteries of their heart. That buildup is an early sign of heart disease. The link held after accounting for other risk factors like age, weight, and blood pressure.

Heart disease is a leading cause of death for Black women in the US, and high blood pressure often starts earlier for us than for other groups.

## Signs stress is getting into your body

- Headaches or a pounding heart when you're upset
- Trouble sleeping
- Clenched jaw or grinding your teeth
- Craving salty or sugary food under pressure
- Feeling keyed up even when nothing is wrong

## Anxiety and the heart

Anxiety can cause chest tightness and a racing heart, and so can heart problems. If you have chest pain, shortness of breath, or pain in your arm or jaw, get checked right away. Black women's heart symptoms are sometimes dismissed as anxiety. You have the right to be examined.

## Try this

- **Check your blood pressure** at a pharmacy or with a home cuff. Write down the numbers for two weeks and share them with your doctor.
- **Name the stress.** After a hard interaction, say or write what happened. Holding it in keeps your body on alert longer.
- **Move every day,** even 10 minutes of walking. Movement helps your heart and your mood.
- **Know heart attack signs in women:** chest discomfort, shortness of breath, nausea, unusual tiredness, and pain in the back, jaw, or arm. Call 911 if they happen.
