A video of another Black person being hurt or killed shows up in your feed. You watch it, or try not to. Either way, it stays with you for days. That reaction has a name: vicarious racial trauma.

## What research shows

> A national study found that after police killed unarmed Black Americans, Black adults living in the same state reported more days of poor mental health in the following months. White adults in the same states did not show that change. A separate study of teens of color found that seeing traumatic race-related events online was linked to more symptoms of depression and post-traumatic stress.

You don't have to be there for it to affect your body. Seeing harm to people who look like you, your brother, or your child tells your nervous system that you are not safe either.

## How it can show up

- Trouble sleeping after a news event
- Feeling on edge in public or around police
- Numbness, or feeling nothing at all
- Anger that comes out at people close to you
- Checking your phone over and over for updates

## Caring for yourself and staying informed

You can care about what is happening without taking in every image. Staying informed and protecting your peace can go together.

## Try this

- **Turn off autoplay** on your social apps so videos don't start without your choice.
- **Read instead of watch.** A written article gives you the facts without replaying the harm.
- **Set a news window.** Check updates once or twice a day at set times, not right before bed.
- **Do one grounding act after hard news.** Step outside, put your feet on the floor, and name five things you can see.
- **Gather.** Talk with people who understand, attend a vigil, or take part in action that matters to you. Collective care eases the weight.
