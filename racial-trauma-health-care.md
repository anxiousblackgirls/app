Many Black women have a story about not being believed by a doctor. Pain brushed off. Symptoms blamed on weight or stress. Concerns dismissed until they became emergencies. Those experiences are more than frustrating. They can be dangerous, and they're a source of trauma.

## What research shows

> CDC data for 2021 showed Black women in the US died from pregnancy-related causes at about 2.6 times the rate of white women. The CDC has reported that most pregnancy-related deaths are preventable. A study of white medical students and residents found that about half believed at least one myth about biological differences between Black and white people, and those who did rated Black patients' pain as lower.

Black women of every income and education level face these risks. Being wealthy or famous doesn't fully protect you.

## How it affects mental health

- Anxiety before medical appointments
- Avoiding the doctor, even when something is wrong
- Fear during pregnancy and childbirth
- Anger, or feeling powerless, after being dismissed
- Distrust that keeps you from getting care you need

That distrust has deep roots, from the Tuskegee study to experiments on enslaved women. Your caution is understandable. Your health still matters.

## Try this

- **Bring an advocate.** A partner, friend, or family member can speak up, take notes, and back you up.
- **Use clear words:** "I'm in severe pain. On a scale of 1 to 10, it's an 8. I need this taken seriously."
- **Ask, "What else could this be?"** and "What would you do if this were your sister?"
- **Request documentation.** If a provider refuses a test or treatment, ask them to note in your chart what you asked for and why they declined.
- **Consider a doula** during pregnancy. Research has linked doula support to better birth outcomes.
- **Find Black providers** when you can. Directories and word of mouth from other Black women help.
