If teachers called you chatty, disrespectful, or "smart but lazy," you may have been showing ADHD. Nobody looked closer.

## How it shows up

ADHD (attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder) affects focus, energy, and self-control. In girls and women it often looks quieter than the classic picture of a boy who can't sit still:

- Daydreaming and drifting off in conversations
- Losing track of time and running late
- Starting ten projects and finishing one
- Talking fast, interrupting, or blurting things out
- A racing mind at night
- Strong emotions that come and go quickly
- Locking in for hours on something interesting, then struggling with boring tasks

Many of us cover ADHD by overworking: staying up late, making endless lists, and doing everything at the last minute under pressure. That works for a while and then wears you down.

> A national study that followed US children from kindergarten to eighth grade found Black children were less likely to be diagnosed with ADHD than white children with similar behavior. Other research shows adults often see Black girls as older and less in need of protection, and Black girls are disciplined at higher rates for the same behavior. What could have been a sign of ADHD was often treated as a behavior problem.

## Why it gets tangled with anxiety

Years of missed deadlines and being told to try harder can build real anxiety and shame on top of ADHD. If anxiety treatment has only helped a little, it may be worth asking whether ADHD is underneath.

## Try this

- **Find your old report cards.** Look for comments like "talks too much," "doesn't finish work," or "daydreams." Circle the ones that still sound like you.
- **List your coping systems.** Write down how you get through the day: alarms, lists, last-minute rushes, extra coffee. A long list is a clue worth bringing to a provider.
- **Try a free screening.** The Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS) is a short screening questionnaire from the World Health Organization. It isn't a diagnosis, but it can start the conversation.
