Infertility can put a strain on your relationship, your family gatherings, and your group chats. Even the people who love you can say the wrong thing.

## How infertility strains relationships

Partners often cope differently. One may want to talk about it all the time while the other goes quiet. Treatment schedules can take the joy out of sex. Money decisions can turn into arguments. Research comparing women with infertility to women with cancer and heart disease found similar levels of anxiety and depression, and that kind of stress lands on a relationship.

Family and friends may ask when you're having kids, share pregnancy news without warning, or offer advice like "just relax." Each comment can sting.

> In a study of Black women with infertility, nearly all coped mostly in silence, even when people close to them knew. Some women with secondary infertility, trouble having another child, said they got little sympathy because they already had one. Many Black families value privacy, and some partners feel shame about infertility, especially when male factors are involved. All of this can leave you managing everyone else's feelings while hiding your own.

## Try this

- **Talk with your partner about how you each cope.** Ask, "What helps you when it's hard? What do you need from me?"
- **Schedule time that has nothing to do with trying.** Date nights, trips, or a show you watch together. Protect your relationship from becoming only about fertility.
- **Decide together what to share.** Agree on who knows, what you'll say, and how to answer questions.
- **Give family a script.** "Please don't ask about kids. We'll share news if there is any." One trusted relative can help spread the word.
- **Ask friends to give you a heads-up** about pregnancy announcements so you can react in private.
- **Consider couples counseling.** A therapist who works with infertility can help you both feel like a team again.
