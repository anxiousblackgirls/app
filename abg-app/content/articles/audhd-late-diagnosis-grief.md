Getting diagnosed as an adult can bring relief and grief at the same time. Relief that there's a reason. Grief for the girl who struggled without help, and for the years you spent thinking something was wrong with you.

## What women describe

> Researchers interviewed women diagnosed as autistic in middle or late adulthood. They described years of feeling different and exhausted from trying to fit in. After diagnosis, many felt relief and a new understanding of their past. Many also described a period of looking back on their lives with new eyes, which included sadness and anger about what they had missed.

## Grief through a Black lens

Many Black women grew up in schools that saw their differences as behavior problems, not as signs of how their brains worked. You may grieve:

- Being labeled "bad," "lazy," or "disrespectful" as a child
- Punishments for things you couldn't control
- Opportunities you missed
- Relationships that ended because no one understood you
- The support your family didn't know to give

All of those feelings are valid. You can love your family and still grieve what you didn't get.

## Moving forward

Diagnosis also gives you something new: a way to understand yourself, ask for what you need, and find community. Many women say life gets easier after the grief settles.

## Try this

- **Rewrite one old label.** Write a word you were called growing up, like "lazy." Next to it, write what was really happening, like "overwhelmed and not getting help."
- **Write a letter to your younger self.** Tell her what you know now. You don't have to share it with anyone.
- **Find others diagnosed late.** Hearing your story in someone else's can ease the loneliness.
- **Give it time.** Grief after diagnosis often comes in waves, and therapy can help you work through it.
