You have heard it your whole life: she's so strong. Strength helped our mothers and grandmothers survive. Expected to be strong all the time, many of us end up exhausted and alone with our pain.

## The Superwoman role

Researcher Cheryl Woods-Giscombé interviewed Black women about stress and found a pattern she called the Superwoman Schema. It includes:

- Showing strength no matter what
- Holding back emotions
- Resisting help or being vulnerable
- Being driven to succeed with limited resources
- Putting others' needs before your own

Women in her study said this role helped them survive and protect their families. They also linked it to stress, poor sleep, and putting off their own health care.

> Later research found that the "strong" part is not the problem by itself. Self-silencing is. Black women who held back their feelings to keep the peace reported more symptoms of depression.

## Keeping the strength, dropping the weight

You do not have to stop being strong. You can redefine it:

- Strength can mean saying "I'm not okay" to someone safe.
- Strength can mean asking for help before you hit empty.
- Strength can mean letting your body rest without guilt.

## Try this

At the end of the day, write down one feeling you held back. Next to it, write what you needed in that moment. Over time this helps you notice your needs before they turn into burnout.
