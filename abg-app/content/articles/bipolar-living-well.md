Bipolar disorder is a lifelong condition, and many people with it live full, steady lives. Treatment and daily habits work together to keep episodes fewer and shorter.

## Treatment

Most people with bipolar disorder do best with a combination of medication and therapy. Mood stabilizers and some other medications help prevent episodes. Therapy helps you spot early warning signs, manage stress, and work through what bipolar disorder has affected in your life. Medication decisions belong with a psychiatrist who knows your history.

## Daily habits that protect you

- **Guard your sleep.** Losing sleep can set off mania. Keep the same bedtime and wake time, even on weekends.
- **Track your mood.** A quick daily note about mood, sleep, and energy helps you and your provider catch changes early.
- **Know your warning signs.** Write down what your highs and lows look like at the start. Share the list with someone you trust.
- **Limit alcohol and drugs.** They make episodes more likely and treatment less effective.

## Try this: make a plan while you're well

When you feel steady, write down who to call, which provider to contact, and what you want people to do if you start getting sick. Give a copy to one or two trusted people. A plan made in a calm moment helps everyone act fast later.

> Words like "crazy" get thrown around in our families and churches, and they keep people quiet. Bipolar disorder is a medical condition like diabetes or high blood pressure. Managing it takes strength, and you deserve people around you who see that.
