Masking means hiding autistic traits to fit in. You might force eye contact, script conversations ahead of time, copy how other people laugh, or hold in the urge to move or fidget.

## Why we mask

People mask to avoid being teased, punished, or left out. Researchers who interviewed autistic adults found many started masking young and kept doing it in almost every setting. They described it as exhausting, and many said it left them unsure who they really were.

> Many Black women already know what it means to change how you talk and carry yourself depending on the room. That is often called code-switching. For a Black autistic woman, masking and code-switching can stack on top of each other, which means double the effort just to get through the day.

## The cost

Masking takes energy every minute. Over time it can lead to:

- Autistic burnout: deep exhaustion, losing skills you usually have, and needing to pull away from everything
- Anxiety and depression
- Feeling like no one knows the real you

## Try this

You do not have to stop masking everywhere. Start where it is safe:

- Notice when you are masking and how tired you feel after.
- Pick one safe place or person where you let yourself stim, skip small talk, or say "that's too loud for me."
- Build recovery time into your week after big social events.
- Use tools without apology: headphones, sunglasses, a quiet corner.
