Medication is one of the most effective treatments for ADHD, and one of the most debated in our community. You may have heard it will change your personality, that it's "the same as drugs," or that it's just a way to sedate Black kids. Here's what research says, so you can decide for yourself.

## Who gets treated

> A large study of US children found that Black children were less likely than white children to be diagnosed with ADHD, and once diagnosed, less likely to be taking ADHD medication. Researchers pointed to family concerns about medication, mistrust of the health system, and differences in how providers offer treatment.

That mistrust has real roots, from the Tuskegee study to ongoing bias in care today. Being careful is reasonable. So is having the facts.

## What to know

- **There are two main kinds of ADHD medication.** Stimulants work for most people. Non-stimulants are an option for people who can't take stimulants or prefer not to.
- **Medication doesn't change who you are.** When the dose is right, people usually feel more like themselves, just more focused.
- **Side effects are common at first** and often include trouble sleeping or lower appetite. A prescriber adjusts the medication to reduce them.
- **Medication is one tool.** Many people combine it with therapy, coaching, and routines. Some choose not to take medication at all.

## Try this

Bring these questions to your prescriber:

- "What are the options, and how are they different?"
- "What side effects should I watch for, and when should I call you?"
- "How will we know if it's working?"
- "Can I take this with my other medications or health conditions?"
- "What can I try besides medication?"

Write down how you feel each day for the first few weeks. Your notes help your prescriber get the dose right.
