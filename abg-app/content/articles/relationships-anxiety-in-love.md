Anxiety does not stay in one part of your life. It follows you into dating, marriage, and friendship, and it can make love feel like something you have to watch closely so you don't lose it.

## How it shows up

- Rereading texts to figure out what they "really" meant
- Panicking when someone takes a while to reply
- Asking for reassurance again and again
- Saying yes when you mean no to keep the peace
- Pulling away first so you can't get hurt
- Staying in relationships that drain you because leaving feels scarier

These patterns often start as protection. If love felt unpredictable growing up, your nervous system learned to scan for signs that it might be taken away.

> Research on Black women found that self-silencing, holding back your feelings and needs to avoid conflict, was linked to more symptoms of depression. Many of us were taught to keep the peace at home and be the easy one. In relationships, that can mean giving everything and asking for nothing.

## Try this

- **Name the pattern.** "My anxiety is telling me they're upset with me" puts space between you and the fear.
- **Check the facts.** Ask what you know for sure, and what you are guessing.
- **Say the need.** "I feel better when we check in once during the day" is clearer than hoping they will guess.
- **Notice how you feel around them.** Calm and safe is a good sign. Constant worry is information too.

Couples therapy and individual therapy both help with relationship anxiety. You deserve love that feels like rest.
