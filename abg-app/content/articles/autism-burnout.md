Autistic burnout is a deep exhaustion that comes from living in a world that wasn't built for your brain. It goes beyond being tired. People describe losing abilities they normally have, like speaking, cooking, or keeping up with work.

## What it feels like

> Researchers who interviewed autistic adults defined autistic burnout as long-term exhaustion, loss of skills, and lower tolerance for sensory input, lasting three months or more. Participants said it came from the build-up of life stress and from the demands of masking. Many said that unmasking, rest, and time alone helped them recover.

Signs can include:

- Being too exhausted to do basic daily tasks
- Losing speech or struggling to find words
- Sensory overload from things that didn't bother you before
- Pulling away from people completely
- Forgetting things and losing track of time

## Why Black women are at higher risk

Many Black autistic women spend their days masking autistic traits and code-switching at the same time. You're managing how you sound, how you move, and how you're seen as a Black woman, on top of the sensory and social demands of autism. Many also carry heavy caregiving and work loads. Without rest, that adds up to burnout.

Autistic burnout can look like depression, and the two can happen together. If you feel hopeless, talk to a provider.

## Try this

- **Do an energy budget.** List your usual activities and mark each one as draining or restoring. Aim to follow every big drain with something restoring.
- **Cut demands, not just add rest.** Pick one task this week to drop, delegate, or do the easy way.
- **Create one unmasked space.** Pick a time, a place, or a person where you don't have to perform.
- **Plan recovery days** after big events like weddings, holidays, and conferences.
