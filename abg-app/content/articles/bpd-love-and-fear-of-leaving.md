With BPD, love can feel like standing on the edge of a cliff. When you feel close to someone, the fear of losing them can be as strong as the love. Small signs, like a short text or a canceled plan, can feel like proof they're leaving.

## Common patterns

- Seeing someone as perfect, then suddenly as cruel
- Testing people to see if they'll stay
- Panic when someone needs space
- Saying things in anger you regret later
- Ending relationships first to avoid being left

These patterns come from fear, not from a lack of love. They often push away the very people you want close.

> Many Black women were taught to need no one. When you also live with BPD, that message collides with a deep need for connection. You may swing between hiding your needs completely and expressing them all at once in a crisis. DBT's interpersonal skills help you find the middle: asking for what you need clearly and calmly.

## DEAR MAN

DEAR MAN is a DBT skill for asking for something or saying no while keeping the relationship and your self-respect.

- **D**escribe the facts. "You said you'd call last night and didn't."
- **E**xpress your feelings. "I felt scared and alone."
- **A**ssert what you want. "I need a text if plans change."
- **R**einforce why it helps. "That way I won't spiral, and we won't fight."
- **M**indful. Stay on your one request. Don't bring up old issues.
- **A**ppear confident, even if you don't feel it.
- **N**egotiate. "Would a quick text work for you?"

## Try this

- **Write a DEAR MAN** for one situation this week before you have the conversation.
- **Pause before you send.** When fear spikes, write the text, then wait 20 minutes before deciding whether to send it.
- **Check the facts.** List three other reasons someone might be quiet besides "they're leaving me."
