If you're AuDHD and Black, you may be managing three performances at once: hiding autistic traits, holding in ADHD restlessness, and code-switching to be accepted in white spaces. That's a lot of energy spent just being in the room.

## Masking and code-switching

> Research on autistic adults found that masking (also called camouflaging) is widespread and exhausting, and is linked to anxiety and a lost sense of self. Separately, research on Black professionals describes code-switching, adjusting speech, appearance, and behavior to fit in at work, as a strategy that can help with professional success but also takes a psychological toll.

For Black AuDHD women, these overlap. You may:

- Force eye contact while also watching your tone so you aren't seen as angry
- Hold still in meetings while your body is begging to move
- Script conversations while also adjusting your language and hair for the room
- Laugh at jokes you don't understand to avoid seeming rude or "difficult"

## The cost

Each layer of masking draws from the same tank. When it runs empty, it can lead to burnout, anxiety, depression, or shutdowns. Many women only realize how much they were masking after they're diagnosed.

## Try this

- **Do a masking inventory.** For one day, notice each time you change how you act. Write down where, how, and how tired you felt after.
- **Choose where to drop a layer.** You may need to code-switch at work, but can you stim freely at home, or skip small talk with friends who get it?
- **Find people who don't need the performance.** Online groups for Black neurodivergent women can be a place to be fully yourself.
- **Build in decompression time** after high-masking situations, like 15 minutes alone in your car before going inside.
