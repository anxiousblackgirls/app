Anxiety is your body's alarm system. It is supposed to switch on when there is danger and switch off when you are safe. With an anxiety disorder, the alarm keeps ringing after the danger has passed, or rings when there was no danger at all.

## How it shows up

Anxiety lives in the body as much as the mind. You might notice:

- A racing heart, tight chest, or shallow breathing
- An upset stomach before work, church, or family events
- Snapping at people when you feel overwhelmed
- Replaying conversations for hours
- Trouble falling asleep because your mind will not slow down
- Planning for every bad outcome "just in case"

Many Black women are high achievers with anxiety. From the outside you look organized and calm. On the inside you are running on fear.

> Researchers call it vigilance: staying on guard because you expect to be treated unfairly. Studies have linked experiences of racial discrimination to higher rates of anxiety, including generalized anxiety disorder, among Black adults in the US. Your body learned to watch for danger for real reasons.

## When to get support

Everyone feels anxious sometimes. It is worth talking to a professional when worry takes up a big part of most days, keeps you from things you want to do, or shows up in your body often. Anxiety is one of the most treatable mental health conditions. Therapy approaches like cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) teach your brain and body that the alarm can come down.

The 5-4-3-2-1 exercise pulls you back into the present. Name 5 things you see, 4 things you can touch, 3 things you hear, 2 things you smell, and 1 thing you taste.
