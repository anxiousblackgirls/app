ADHD isn't only about attention. Many people with ADHD feel emotions fast and intensely: frustration that flares in seconds, hurt that hits hard after criticism, excitement that takes over.

## What the research says

> A major review found that trouble managing emotions is common in ADHD and is now seen by many researchers as a core part of the condition for a large share of people, not just a side effect. It can show up as irritability, quick temper, and getting overwhelmed easily.

## Why it hits Black women differently

When a Black woman shows strong emotion, people are quick to call her angry, aggressive, or "too much." That stereotype can follow you into school, work, and doctors' offices. If ADHD makes your emotions faster and bigger, you may have spent years being punished for reactions you couldn't easily control, and learned to hide everything. Hiding takes a lot of energy and can turn into anxiety or shame.

## Understanding yourself

Strong emotions don't make you a bad person. They often come with real strengths: passion, empathy, and energy for what matters to you. The goal is to create a little space between feeling and reacting.

## Try this

- **Name it to tame it.** When you feel a surge, say the emotion in your head: "This is frustration." Naming an emotion helps calm it.
- **Use a 90-second pause.** Before responding, breathe and wait about a minute and a half. The first spike often settles in that time.
- **Have an exit line ready.** "I want to finish this conversation. Give me ten minutes." Leaving to calm down is a skill, not a failure.
- **Check your basics.** Hunger, poor sleep, and missed medication all make emotions bigger. Fix those first when you can.
