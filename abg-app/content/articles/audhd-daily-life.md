Living with AuDHD means building a life that respects your need for structure and your need for variety at the same time. These ideas come from what many AuDHD people report helps. Try what fits and drop what doesn't.

## Structure with room to move

- **Loose routines.** Keep the order of your morning the same, but let the details change. Same steps, different breakfast.
- **Visual plans.** A whiteboard or phone widget with today's three priorities beats a long list you never look at.
- **Body doubling.** Working next to another person, in person or on video, helps many people start and finish tasks.
- **Timers.** Short work blocks with a reward at the end help with focus and with knowing when to stop.

## Protect your senses

Keep a small kit with you: earplugs or headphones, sunglasses, a fidget, a snack, and a soft layer. Leave events early without guilt when your body says it is done.

> Some everyday parts of Black life can be a lot for an AuDHD body: long church services, loud family gatherings, and hours in the chair getting hair braided or styled. You can still enjoy these. Bring headphones to the salon, sit near the exit at church, and plan a quiet day after the cookout.

## Plan for recovery

AuDHD burnout often comes from too many demands with too little rest. Put recovery time on your calendar after big days the same way you would put an appointment. Tell the people close to you what recovery looks like for you, so they know it is not about them.

## Try this

- **Build your sensory kit this week** and keep it in your bag or car.
- **Pick one loose routine** for your mornings: same three steps in the same order, with room to change the details.
- **Book a body-doubling session** with a friend for one task you've been putting off.
