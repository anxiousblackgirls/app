"What goes on in this house stays in this house." Many of us grew up with that rule. It protected families when outsiders could not be trusted. It can also make it hard to talk about anxiety, depression, ADHD, or autism with the people closest to you.

## Before you talk

- **Decide your goal.** Do you want understanding, practical help, or just to stop hiding? Your goal shapes what you share.
- **Choose your person.** Start with the family member most likely to listen, not the one whose approval you want most.
- **Pick the moment.** A calm one-on-one beats a holiday dinner.

## Words you can use

- "I've been dealing with anxiety. I'm getting help, and I wanted you to know."
- "I'm not asking you to fix it. I just need you to listen."
- "This isn't about how you raised me. It's about taking care of my health."
- "I got diagnosed with ADHD. It explains a lot about how I was in school."

> Older relatives may respond with "just pray," "we didn't have that back then," or "you're too blessed to be stressed." Often that comes from fear or from never being given words for their own struggles. You can honor their love and still hold your ground.

## Boundaries are allowed

If a conversation turns into blame or jokes, you can end it: "I'm going to stop here. We can talk another time." You do not need everyone to agree with your care for it to be right for you.

Some families come around slowly. Some never fully do. Your healing does not have to wait for their permission.

## Try this

- **Pick your person and your sentence.** Choose one family member and one sentence from above. Write them down.
- **Practice it out loud** in the mirror or with a friend first.
- **Plan your exit line** in case the conversation goes badly, so you can leave with your peace intact.
