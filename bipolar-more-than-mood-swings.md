Everyone has ups and downs. Bipolar disorder is different. It causes episodes of extreme mood and energy that last days or weeks and change how you sleep, think, and act.

## The two sides

**Mania or hypomania** can look like:

- Needing very little sleep and not feeling tired
- Racing thoughts and talking fast
- Feeling unstoppable, or unusually irritable
- Big spending, risky decisions, or starting many projects at once

Hypomania is a milder form. It can feel good, even productive, which is one reason people don't seek help during it.

**Depression** in bipolar disorder looks like the depression most people know: low mood, exhaustion, losing interest in things, and sometimes thoughts of suicide.

There are different types. Bipolar I includes at least one full manic episode. Bipolar II includes hypomania and depression. Many people are first diagnosed with depression alone because they seek help during a low, not a high.

> Studies of US psychiatric patients found that Black patients with mood disorders, including bipolar disorder, were more likely than white patients to be misdiagnosed with schizophrenia. Researchers have called for better training and fairer assessment. If a diagnosis doesn't fit your experience, you have the right to ask for a second opinion.

## Why the right diagnosis matters

Treatment for bipolar disorder is different from treatment for depression alone. Some antidepressants taken without a mood stabilizer can trigger mania in people with bipolar disorder. Tell your provider about any periods of very high energy or little sleep, even if they felt good at the time.

## Try this

- **Make a mood timeline.** Write down the biggest highs and lows you remember from the last few years: when they happened, how long they lasted, and how much you slept.
- **Bring it to your provider** and ask, "Could this be bipolar disorder rather than depression alone?"
- **Ask for a second opinion** if you were given a diagnosis like schizophrenia and it doesn't match your experience.

If you are having thoughts of suicide, call or text 988 right now.
