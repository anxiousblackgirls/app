Having a baby is supposed to be joyful. For many new mothers it is also exhausting, lonely, and frightening. When sadness, anxiety, or numbness last longer than a couple of weeks after birth, it may be postpartum depression.

## Signs to watch for

- Feeling sad, empty, or hopeless most of the day
- Intense worry or panic about the baby
- Trouble bonding with your baby
- Feeling like you're failing as a mother
- Sleeping poorly even when the baby sleeps
- Pulling away from family and friends
- Thoughts of harming yourself or your baby

The "baby blues" are common in the first two weeks and fade on their own. Postpartum depression lasts longer, feels heavier, and can start any time in the first year.

> A study of low-income mothers found that Black and Latina women were less likely than white women to start treatment for postpartum depression, and less likely to keep getting care once they started. Many Black mothers also carry fear of being judged or of child protective services getting involved if they speak up. Black women also face higher risks during and after pregnancy in the US health care system, which adds to the stress of the postpartum months.

## You deserve care too

Postpartum depression is not a sign you are a bad mother. It is a medical condition, and it is treatable with therapy, support, and sometimes medication that is safe while breastfeeding.

## Try this

- **Call or text the National Maternal Mental Health Hotline** at 1-833-852-6262 (1-833-TLC-MAMA). It is free, confidential, and open 24/7.
- **Ask for one specific kind of help.** "Can you hold the baby for two hours Saturday so I can sleep?" is easier to say yes to than "let me know if you need anything."
- **Tell your OB or your baby's pediatrician.** Pediatricians screen mothers too, and they can connect you with care.
- **Look for Black maternal support groups** through your doula, hospital, or community organizations.

If you have thoughts of harming yourself or your baby, call 911 or call/text 988 or go to the nearest emergency room now.
