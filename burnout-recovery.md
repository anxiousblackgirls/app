You can't heal burnout by working harder at rest. Recovery has a few specific parts, and research shows which ones make the biggest difference.

## The four parts of recovery

> Work psychologists Sabine Sonnentag and Charlotte Fritz identified four experiences that help people recover from work stress: psychological detachment (mentally switching off from work), relaxation, mastery (doing something challenging that you choose, like learning a skill), and control (deciding how you spend your free time). People who got more of these in their off hours reported less exhaustion and better well-being.

## Why recovery is hard for Black women

Many of us never fully clock out. Work follows us home through our phones. Family, church, and community needs fill our weekends. Rest can feel lazy or even unsafe, especially if you've been taught you have to work harder to keep your place. Detachment can feel risky when your job feels uncertain.

## Recovery is a skill

Like any skill, it gets easier with practice. Start small and protect it.

## Try this

Plan one recovery action for each of the four parts this week:

- **Detach:** Turn off work email notifications after a set time. Change clothes when you get home as a signal that work is over.
- **Relax:** Take a bath, listen to music, sit outside, or nap without guilt.
- **Mastery:** Do something just for you that takes focus: a dance class, a new recipe, learning to braid, painting.
- **Control:** Choose one evening or morning that belongs only to you. Say no to anything that tries to take it.

Then check in after a week. Which one helped most? Do more of that.

If you still feel empty or hopeless after several weeks of real rest, it may be depression. Reach out to a therapist or doctor.
