If your ADHD feels worse at certain times of the month, or got harder in your 40s, you're not imagining it. Hormones affect ADHD symptoms in women, and research is only starting to catch up.

## Your cycle and your focus

> A study tracking women through their menstrual cycle found that ADHD symptoms shifted with hormone levels. Lower estrogen was linked to more impulsivity, especially in women with higher ADHD traits. Many women report that focus, mood, and organization get harder in the week before their period.

## Perimenopause and menopause

Estrogen drops during perimenopause, the years leading up to menopause. Many women say their ADHD symptoms worsen, and some are first diagnosed in this stage of life.

A large study of women going through menopause found that Black women had the longest stretch of hot flashes and night sweats, lasting about ten years on average. Those symptoms disrupt sleep, and poor sleep makes ADHD symptoms worse. Black women also report being dismissed when they bring menopause symptoms to doctors.

## Why this matters

If your ADHD treatment works some weeks and not others, hormones may be part of the reason. Your provider can only adjust for that if they know.

## Try this

- **Track your cycle and your symptoms together** for two or three months. Each day, rate focus, mood, and energy from 1 to 5 next to your cycle day.
- **Plan lighter weeks.** If the week before your period is harder, schedule demanding tasks for other weeks when you can.
- **Bring your tracking to your provider** and ask, "My ADHD gets worse at certain points in my cycle. Can we talk about that?"
- **Ask about perimenopause** if you're in your late 30s or 40s and symptoms have changed.
