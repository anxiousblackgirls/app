"Black don't crack" may be true on the outside. On the inside, research tells a different story. Years of stress from racism, money worries, and caregiving can wear down Black women's bodies earlier than they should.

## What weathering means

> Public health researcher Arline Geronimus calls this "weathering." Her team measured allostatic load, a score built from signs of chronic stress like blood pressure, blood sugar, and inflammation. Black women had higher scores than white women and Black men, and the gap grew with age. The difference held even when comparing people with similar incomes. Being Black and a woman carried a stress cost of its own.

## What it can look like

- High blood pressure in your 30s or 40s
- Constant tension in your neck, jaw, or shoulders
- Getting sick often
- Headaches and stomach problems
- Feeling tired in a way sleep doesn't fix
- Feeling older than your age

## Why this matters for mental health

Chronic stress and mental health feed each other. Stress raises the risk of anxiety and depression, and anxiety and depression keep your stress system switched on. Caring for your mind is also caring for your heart, your blood pressure, and your future.

## This is not your fault

Weathering comes from conditions, like discrimination, unsafe neighborhoods, and carrying too much without support. You can't fix those alone. You can protect yourself, build support, and push for change alongside others.

## Try this

- **Know your numbers.** Get your blood pressure, blood sugar, and cholesterol checked this year, and ask what they mean for you.
- **Do one daily release.** Five minutes of slow breathing, a walk, prayer, or stretching tells your stress system it can stand down.
- **Spend time with your people.** Connection with others who understand you is one of the strongest buffers against stress.
- **Look at one source of chronic stress** and ask: is there one thing I can change, share, or let go of?
