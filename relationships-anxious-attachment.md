Attachment style describes the way you connect with the people closest to you. If you have an anxious attachment style, closeness can feel like something you could lose at any moment, so you work hard to hold on.

## What anxious attachment looks like

> Researchers who first applied attachment theory to adult romantic relationships found that the patterns people formed with caregivers as children often showed up again in adult love. People with an anxious style worried about being abandoned, wanted more closeness than partners gave, and felt intense highs and lows in relationships.

In daily life it can look like:

- Needing frequent reassurance that your partner still cares
- Reading a lot into short texts or a change in tone
- Feeling panicked when someone pulls back, even a little
- Doing more and more to keep someone happy
- Staying in relationships that hurt because being alone feels worse

## How it shows up for Black women

Many Black women learned early that love could be inconsistent, that people leave, or that you had to earn your place by being useful. Add the Strong Black Woman message and you may swing between two extremes: hiding every need so you don't seem "needy," then feeling flooded with fear when the relationship feels shaky. Research on the Strong Black Woman role found that many women felt they had to keep up an image of strength even in close relationships, and that this made it hard to ask for support.

Attachment styles can change. With safe relationships and practice, many people become more secure over time.

## Try this

- **Soothe first, then text.** When panic rises, take five slow breaths and put a hand on your chest before you reach for your phone.
- **Say the need plainly.** "I feel more secure when we check in once a day" works better than testing someone to see if they'll notice.
- **Watch actions over time,** not single moments. One slow reply is not a pattern.
- **Notice who feels calm to be around.** Secure people can feel boring at first to an anxious heart. Give steady love a chance.
