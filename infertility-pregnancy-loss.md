A miscarriage can happen before anyone else knew you were pregnant, and then there's no one to grieve with. Some women go back to work the next day and act like nothing happened. That kind of loss deserves space.

## Loss is common, and Black women face higher risk

Miscarriage is the most common pregnancy loss. In a U.S. study that followed more than 4,000 pregnant women, Black women had a higher risk of miscarriage than white women, especially later in the first trimester, even after adjusting for age and alcohol use. The researchers couldn't fully explain the difference, which means it isn't something you caused.

## Grief that goes unseen

Grief expert Kenneth Doka coined the term "disenfranchised grief" for losses that others don't recognize or make room for. Pregnancy loss is a common example. People may say "at least it was early" or "you can try again," as if the loss doesn't count. It does.

> Many Black women grieve pregnancy loss in silence. We may not want to hear "God has a plan" or be told to stay strong. We may worry about how family will react, or feel we have to protect a partner who's hurting too. In research on Black women with infertility, many kept their pain private, partly because of expectations to be strong and self-reliant. Holding grief in can turn into anxiety, depression, and fear about future pregnancies.

## Try this

- **Call it what it is.** A loss. You can name your baby, keep an ultrasound photo, or plant something in their memory if that feels right.
- **Tell people what you need.** "I don't need advice. I just need you to check on me this week."
- **Take time off if you can.** Your body and mind both need recovery.
- **Expect hard dates.** Due dates and anniversaries can bring grief back. Plan something gentle for those days.
- **Ask your doctor questions.** If you've had more than one loss, ask whether testing makes sense.
- **Get support.** Pregnancy loss support groups and therapists who specialize in reproductive grief can help. You can also call or text the National Maternal Mental Health Hotline at 1-833-852-6262, 24/7.
