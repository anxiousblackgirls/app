For a long time, people said Black women were "protected" from body image problems because our culture loved curves. Then came the slim-thick era: snatched waist, flat stomach, big hips, round behind, all at once. Plenty of us now feel pressure to have a body that very few people are born with.

## Black women feel body pressure too

The idea that Black women don't struggle with body image doesn't hold up. A review of 98 studies with about 42,000 women found that white women were only slightly more dissatisfied with their bodies than Black women. The ideal we're measured against may look different, curvier and less about being thin, but the pressure to match it is still there. And when the ideal is a waist-to-hip ratio most bodies can't reach without surgery, a lot of women end up feeling "wrong."

## The BBL question

Social media made the Brazilian butt lift look as routine as getting your nails done. It isn't. In 2017, a task force of plastic surgeons estimated that between 1 in 2,351 and 1 in 6,214 of these procedures ended in death, and said the true rate was likely higher. That made it one of the deadliest cosmetic surgeries. Surgeons have since changed techniques to make it safer, but risks remain, especially with discount clinics, travel packages and unlicensed providers. Whatever you decide about your body is your choice. Make it with full information and a board-certified plastic surgeon.

> Our bodies have been put on display and judged for centuries, from Sarah Baartman being exhibited in Europe to music videos today. Loving curves can be a beautiful part of our culture. It turns into pressure when your worth depends on how closely your body matches the one trending this year.

## Body appreciation helps

Researchers who study positive body image describe it as appreciating what your body can do, accepting the parts you don't love and filtering out messages that make you feel worse. You can dislike parts of your body and still treat it with respect while you live in it.

## Try this

- **Clean up your feed.** Unfollow or mute accounts that leave you comparing. Follow women with bodies like yours.
- **Notice the edits.** Filters, angles, shapewear and surgery shape most of the bodies you see online.
- **Thank your body for one thing a day.** "My legs got me through that walk." "My arms held my niece."
- **Ask what you're trying to fix.** If you're considering surgery, ask yourself what you hope it will change in your life, and talk it through with someone you trust first.
- **Talk back to the voice.** When you catch yourself picking your body apart in the mirror, ask, "Would I say this to my best friend?"
