Single Black mothers have been talked about for decades, usually by people who never walked a day in their shoes. The real story includes stress, money worries, and exhaustion. It also includes skill, love, and a lot of creativity.

## The stereotypes are part of the stress

Patricia Hill Collins described "controlling images," stereotypes of Black women that shape how others treat us. Images like the "welfare mother" paint single Black mothers as irresponsible. Those images ignore the reasons women end up parenting alone, and they can follow you into schools, doctors' offices, and job interviews.

> Many single Black mothers carry an extra load: proving they're good mothers to a world that already assumed otherwise. That can look like over-preparing for every school meeting, never letting anyone see you struggle, and feeling guilty for any time spent on yourself. Holding up that front takes energy you need for your kids and your own health.

## What wears on single mothers

A study of 210 Black single mothers asked them what made them feel depressed. The most common answer was not having enough money. General stress came next, followed by the daily hassles of parenting. Physical health was mentioned least. The researchers concluded that the strain came mostly from life circumstances, not from something wrong with the mothers, and called for more financial and social support.

Keep that in mind when you judge yourself. If you're worn out, it's because you're carrying a lot, often without enough help.

## Try this

- **Separate guilt from responsibility.** Ask, "Did I do something wrong, or am I just stretched thin?" Most of the time, it's the second one.
- **Build a short list of backup people.** Two or three people you can call for pickup, a meal, or a break. Trade favors with other parents.
- **Use the resources that exist.** Child care subsidies, school programs, and community groups are there for families like yours.
- **Protect small pockets of rest.** Ten quiet minutes after bedtime counts.
- **Talk to your kids at their level.** Children handle hard truths better when they hear them simply and honestly.
- **Get support for you.** Therapy, a support group, or a friend who listens without judging can make the load lighter.
