"Black don't crack" is a compliment we hear our whole lives. Getting older brings more than how you look, though. It brings changes in health, roles, relationships, and how the world sees you.

## What research shows

> Public health researcher Arline Geronimus found that stress from racism and hardship causes Black women's bodies to age faster on the inside, with more signs of wear on the heart, metabolism, and immune system by middle age than white women of the same age. Pew Research Center has described midlife adults caring for both aging parents and children as the "sandwich generation," a role many Black women fill.

So while you may look young, your body may need more care than people assume, and earlier.

## What aging can bring

- Health changes like high blood pressure, diabetes, or joint pain
- Perimenopause and menopause
- Caring for aging parents while still raising children or grandchildren
- Grief as parents, elders, and friends pass
- Questions about purpose after children leave or work ends
- Feeling invisible in a culture that values youth

## The gifts of aging

Many Black women say they care less about what others think as they age. They speak more freely, rest more, and know who they are. Our elders also carry wisdom and respect in our communities.

## Try this

- **Schedule your checkups.** Blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol, mammograms, and bone health. Put them on the calendar this year.
- **Plan for your own care,** not just everyone else's. Think about savings, health insurance, and who will support you as you age.
- **Talk with an elder** you admire. Ask what they wish they'd known at your age.
- **Write what you want this next season to hold.** Travel, learning, rest, faith, love, or something new.
