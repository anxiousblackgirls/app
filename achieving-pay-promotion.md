You do the work of the job above you. You train new hires who end up promoted past you. You find out a coworker with less experience makes more. If that makes you angry, it should. Black women are paid less and promoted less, even when we ask just as often.

## The numbers

In 2023, Black women working full time, year round were paid 66 cents for every dollar paid to white, non-Hispanic men, according to the National Women's Law Center. Over a career, that adds up to hundreds of thousands of dollars.

Promotions follow the same pattern. Lean In found that for every 100 men promoted to their first manager role, only 58 Black women were promoted, even though Black women asked for promotions as often as men. That first step matters most, because it decides who's in line for every step after.

## Why asking can feel so hard

Many of us were raised to be grateful for any job, to keep our heads down and to let our work speak for itself. Daughters of immigrants often carry extra pressure: our parents sacrificed so much, so how can we complain? And Afro-Latinas and others who speak more than one language often use those skills at work without being paid for them. Asking for money can feel greedy or risky. Asking is a normal part of the job.

> In many of our families, talking about money was private or even rude. That silence keeps pay gaps hidden. Sharing salary information with trusted friends and coworkers is one of the simplest ways Black women can protect each other.

## Try this

- **Know your number.** Look up salary ranges for your role and city, and ask peers you trust what they make.
- **Keep a wins file.** Write down results, praise and extra duties all year, so you're ready when it's time to ask.
- **Practice the ask out loud.** "Based on my results and the market rate for this role, I'm asking for a salary of ____."
- **Ask what it takes.** "What specifically would I need to show to be promoted in the next cycle?" Get the answer in writing.
- **Get paid for extra skills.** If you translate, mentor or lead diversity work, ask how it's counted in your pay and reviews.
- **Remember you can say no.** If an offer doesn't respect your worth, it's okay to keep looking.
