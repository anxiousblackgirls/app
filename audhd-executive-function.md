Executive function is the set of mental skills you use to plan, start tasks, switch between them, remember steps, and manage time. Both autism and ADHD affect these skills, in overlapping but different ways.

## What research shows

> A review comparing autism, ADHD, and people who have both found executive function difficulties in all three groups. People with both conditions often had a combination of challenges: ADHD-related trouble with attention and impulse control, plus autism-related trouble with flexibility and switching between tasks.

## How it shows up in daily life

- Knowing exactly what you need to do and still not being able to start
- Getting stuck when plans change
- Losing hours to one task and forgetting everything else
- Bills, forms, and appointments piling up
- Feeling "lazy" when you're actually overwhelmed

## The cost for Black women

Many Black women carry heavy expectations at home, at work, at church, and in their extended family. When executive function is hard, those expectations can feel impossible, and failing at them can feel like a moral failure. It's not. Your brain needs different tools, not more willpower.

## Try this

- **Put your brain outside your head.** Use a whiteboard, phone widgets, or sticky notes on the door. If you can't see it, it doesn't exist.
- **Break tasks into the tiniest first step.** Not "clean the kitchen," but "put three dishes in the sink."
- **Body double.** Call a friend on video while you both do boring tasks, or join an online coworking room.
- **Use transition warnings.** Set an alarm ten minutes before you need to switch tasks, so the change doesn't catch you off guard.
- **Automate what you can.** Autopay bills, recurring calendar reminders, and grocery delivery save your energy for what matters.
