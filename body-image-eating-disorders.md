When most people picture an eating disorder, they picture a thin white teenage girl. That picture leaves a lot of Black women out, including women who are struggling right now and have never been asked about it.

## Eating disorders affect Black women too

Eating disorders include binge eating, bulimia (bingeing followed by vomiting, laxatives or overexercising), anorexia and patterns that don't fit neatly into one box. They can happen at any size. An analysis of more than 2,000 girls followed through adolescence found that Black girls were more likely than white girls to show bulimic behaviors. Yet in national data, white teens were more likely to be diagnosed with an eating disorder.

## Why it often gets missed

In one study, clinicians read the same case of a girl with disordered eating. When she was described as Black, they were much less likely to recognize her eating as a problem than when she was described as white. Another study found that women of color with eating disorder symptoms were less likely than white women to be asked about those symptoms by a doctor. When nobody asks, a lot of women assume it's just "stress eating," willpower or a weight problem.

## Signs to pay attention to

- Eating large amounts of food while feeling out of control, then feeling ashamed
- Making yourself throw up, or using laxatives or exercise to "make up" for eating
- Constant thoughts about food, weight or your body that crowd out other things
- Skipping meals or cutting out foods out of fear, not preference
- Eating in secret, or hiding food

> Food in our families is love, celebration and comfort: Sunday dinner, the cookout, Grandma's plate. That can make it hard to admit food has become a struggle. Some of us also learned to eat our feelings because there was no room to say them out loud. Needing help with food doesn't take anything away from your culture or your strength.

## Try this

- **Tell one person.** A friend, therapist or doctor. Say, "I've been struggling with food and I want help."
- **Ask directly at your next appointment:** "Can you screen me for an eating disorder?"
- **Call the National Alliance for Eating Disorders helpline** at 866-662-1235 for free support and help finding treatment.
- **Notice your feelings before you eat.** Pause and ask, "What am I feeling right now?" without judging the answer.
- **Look for a provider who understands Black women** and doesn't make your weight the whole conversation.

If you're thinking about hurting yourself, call or text 988.
