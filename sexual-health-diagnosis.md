The results came back positive. Maybe it's herpes, HPV, HIV or something your provider said can be cured with a round of medicine. Either way, your mind might be racing: Who gave it to me? Who do I have to tell? Will anyone ever want me? Take a breath. An STI diagnosis is a health condition, not a measure of your worth.

## You're far from alone

Sexually transmitted infections are very common. CDC data from 2015–2016 found that about one in eight people ages 14 to 49 had HSV-2, the virus that usually causes genital herpes. The rate was about one in three among Black Americans, and most people with herpes don't know they have it. HPV is so common that nearly everyone who is sexually active will get it at some point. Chlamydia, gonorrhea and trichomoniasis can be cured with medicine.

## About HIV

Black women make up half of new HIV diagnoses among women in the United States. HIV is now a manageable condition. With treatment, many people reach an "undetectable" viral load, and people who stay undetectable do not pass HIV to partners through sex. Partners can also take PrEP to protect themselves.

## The feelings are real

Shame, anger, fear and grief are all common after a diagnosis. Some women replay every relationship. Some worry about their church, family or friends finding out. These feelings usually ease with time, information and support.

> Many of us grew up with silence or shame around sex. "Fast" girls, "nasty" girls, "you better not come home pregnant." Those messages can make an STI feel like proof that something is wrong with you. It isn't. People of every background get STIs, including married women, church women and women who have had one partner.

## Try this

- **Get the facts from your provider.** Ask: "Is this curable or treatable? How do I take care of myself? How do I protect my partners?"
- **Tell partners when you're ready.** Many health departments can notify partners for you without using your name.
- **Use a simple script.** "Before things go further, I want to share something about my health. I have ___. Here's what it means and how we can stay safe."
- **Find support.** Talking with a therapist, a trusted friend or an online community for people with your diagnosis can help.
- **Be gentle with yourself.** Try [Self-compassion break](#skill-self-compassion-break).
