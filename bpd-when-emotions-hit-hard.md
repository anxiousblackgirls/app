Borderline personality disorder (BPD) is a condition where emotions hit fast and hard and take a long time to settle. It affects how you see yourself and how you connect with other people.

## Common signs

- Intense fear of being abandoned, and working hard to avoid it
- Relationships that swing between closeness and conflict
- A sense of self that shifts, or not knowing who you really are
- Acting on impulse in ways that hurt you, like spending or reckless driving
- Self-harm or thoughts of suicide
- Mood shifts that last hours, not weeks
- Feeling empty
- Anger that feels hard to control
- Feeling detached or "not real" under stress

Many people with BPD have lived through trauma, neglect, or unstable relationships growing up. BPD is not a character flaw. It is a pattern your mind learned to protect you.

> Black women already get labeled "angry" or "dramatic" for normal emotions. That stereotype can work both ways in a doctor's office. Real BPD can be brushed off as attitude, and normal reactions to racism or stress can be mislabeled as a disorder. A good evaluation looks at your whole history and context.

## There is hope

BPD was once seen as hard to treat. That has changed. In a study that followed people with BPD for ten years, most reached a point where their symptoms eased enough to no longer meet the diagnosis. Dialectical behavior therapy (DBT), created for BPD, teaches skills for handling emotions, relationships, and crises.

## Try this

- **Track the wave.** Next time a strong emotion hits, note the time it started and when it eased. Seeing that emotions pass helps you ride them out.
- **Write a coping card.** List three things that help you calm down and two people you can call. Keep it in your phone notes.
- **Search for DBT near you** or online, and write down one program to contact this week.

If you are thinking about hurting yourself, call 911 or call/text 988 right now, or go to the Help tab.
