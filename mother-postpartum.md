After a baby arrives, everyone asks about the baby. Fewer people ask how you're doing. For Black mothers, the months after birth can bring real risks to both body and mind, and getting heard can take effort.

## The numbers

In 2024, Black women in the U.S. died from pregnancy-related causes at about three times the rate of white women, according to the CDC. Many of these deaths happen in the weeks and months after birth, and many are preventable.

Mental health care is also uneven. A study of more than 29,000 new mothers on Medicaid found that 4 percent of Black mothers started mental health treatment after giving birth, compared with 9 percent of white mothers. Black mothers who did start were also less likely to get follow-up care.

> Many Black mothers walk into postpartum care already on guard. In the national Giving Voice to Mothers study, women of color reported more mistreatment during pregnancy and birth, including being ignored, shouted at, or having requests for help refused. Add the pressure to be strong, and it makes sense that so many of us downplay how we feel. Postpartum depression and anxiety are medical conditions. Having them doesn't make you a bad mother, and saying so out loud is how you get help.

## Signs to take seriously

- Sadness, hopelessness, or numbness that lasts more than two weeks.
- Constant worry, racing thoughts, or panic.
- Scary thoughts you can't shake, or feeling disconnected from your baby.
- Not sleeping even when the baby sleeps.
- Physical warning signs like a severe headache, chest pain, trouble breathing, or swelling. These need emergency care.

## Try this

- **Say it plainly.** "I just had a baby and I'm not okay. I need to be checked for postpartum depression."
- **Bring a support person** to appointments who can speak up if you're dismissed.
- **Use the National Maternal Mental Health Hotline.** Call or text 1-833-852-6262 (1-833-TLC-MAMA), 24/7, free and confidential.
- **Accept specific help.** When people offer, give them a job: a meal, a load of laundry, two hours of baby holding so you can sleep.
- **Look for a Black doula or therapist** if that would help you feel safer.

If you're thinking about hurting yourself or your baby, call 911 or call or text 988.
