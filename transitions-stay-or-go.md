Sunday nights feel heavy. You've tried speaking up, switching teams and keeping your head down, and the job still costs more than it pays. Deciding whether to stay, leave or build something of your own is a big decision, and it deserves more than a 2 a.m. spiral.

## You're not the only one weighing it

Many Black professionals reach this point. In a 2019 study, 58 percent of Black professionals said they had experienced racial prejudice at work, compared with 15 percent of white professionals, and Black professionals were more likely than white professionals to say they planned to leave to start their own businesses. Many do. Businesses owned by Black women grew 50 percent from 2014 to 2019, faster than any other group of women-owned businesses.

## Questions to ask yourself

- Is the problem this manager, this company or this whole field?
- What would need to change for me to stay, and is that likely?
- What is this job giving me right now: money, skills, insurance, a title?
- How is my body doing? Am I sleeping, eating and feeling like myself?

## Leaving takes a plan

Leaving doesn't have to mean quitting tomorrow. You can start looking quietly, save an emergency fund, build your network, take a class or test a side business while you still have a paycheck. Planning can make leaving feel less scary, and it gives you choices.

> Many of us carry the message that leaving a good job is ungrateful, especially if our parents fought for every opportunity we have. But our families wanted us to thrive. Choosing your peace and your health can honor what they sacrificed. So can staying, if staying is a strategy you chose with your eyes open.

## Try this

- **Write two lists.** What this job gives you, and what it costs you. Look at both after a good day and after a bad day.
- **Set a check-in date.** "If nothing has changed by March, I start applying." A date turns spinning into a plan.
- **Talk to someone who left.** Ask a Black woman who changed jobs or started a business what she wishes she'd known.
- **Build your cushion.** Even a small emergency fund makes every option feel safer.
- **Get support.** A career coach or therapist can help you sort fear from facts. See [Layoffs, career changes and starting over](#article-transitions-career-and-layoffs).
