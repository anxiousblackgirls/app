If you have PCOS and you've been feeling anxious, low, or not like yourself, you're not imagining a connection. PCOS affects mood, and researchers now treat mental health as part of the condition.

## What the research shows

A review of 30 studies found that women with PCOS were about four times as likely to have depressive symptoms and more than five times as likely to have anxiety symptoms as women without PCOS. The link held even when researchers compared women of similar weight, so it isn't only about body image. Hormones, insulin resistance, poor sleep, and the stress of living with visible symptoms all seem to play a part.

Because of this, the 2023 international PCOS guideline recommends that doctors screen every woman with PCOS for depression and anxiety, and ask about eating concerns too.

> Most PCOS studies have included few Black women, so we know less about how anxiety and depression show up for us. What we do know is that many Black women carry the expectation to stay strong and keep going no matter what. Researcher Cheryl Woods-Giscombé described this as the Superwoman Schema: feeling obligated to show strength, hide emotions, and push through without help. When you add a health condition that affects your body and mood, that pressure can turn into quiet suffering. Feeling overwhelmed by PCOS is a reason to ask for support, and asking takes nothing away from your strength.

## Signs to watch for

- Feeling sad, numb, or irritable most days for two weeks or more.
- Constant worry, racing thoughts, or panic.
- Pulling away from people because of how you look or feel.
- Changes in sleep, appetite, or energy that don't lift.

## Try this

- **Name it at your appointment.** "I have PCOS, and I've been struggling with my mood. Can we talk about that too?"
- **Treat sleep as treatment.** PCOS is linked to sleep problems, and poor sleep makes anxiety worse. If you snore or wake up exhausted, ask about sleep apnea.
- **Move in ways you enjoy.** Regular movement helps both insulin resistance and mood. A walk counts.
- **Find a therapist who gets it.** Cognitive behavioral therapy helps with anxiety and depression, and a therapist who understands chronic illness can help with the frustration that comes with PCOS.

If you're having thoughts of hurting yourself, call 911 or call or text 988.
