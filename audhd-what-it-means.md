AuDHD is a community term for being both autistic and ADHD. It is not a separate diagnosis. It describes people who meet criteria for both, and it is more common than most people realize.

## How common it is

Until 2013, the main diagnostic manual used in the US did not allow doctors to diagnose autism and ADHD in the same person. The current manual (DSM-5) changed that. Researchers now estimate that a large share of autistic people also have ADHD, with studies reporting a wide range depending on how they measured it.

## How the two mix

Autism and ADHD can pull in opposite directions:

- Autism may crave routine. ADHD gets bored with routine.
- Autism may need quiet. ADHD may seek stimulation.
- Autism may want to plan every detail. ADHD makes planning hard.

Many AuDHD people describe feeling like two people in one body. Each condition can also hide the other. Your ADHD might make you seem too social to be autistic. Your autistic need for structure might cover your ADHD struggles.

> Black girls are already less likely to be diagnosed with either condition. Having both can make it even harder to get the full picture. If you were diagnosed with one and treatment only helped part of the way, ask about the other.

## Getting evaluated

Look for a clinician who evaluates both autism and ADHD in adults, ideally with experience assessing women and Black clients. Bring examples from childhood and adulthood for both sets of traits.

## Try this

- **Make a two-column list.** Label one side "needs routine, quiet, predictability" and the other "needs variety, movement, stimulation." Write where you see yourself in each. If both columns fill up, bring the list to a clinician.
- **Notice the tug-of-war.** For one week, write down moments when you wanted two opposite things at once. Those moments are useful information.
- **Ask about both** if you have one diagnosis: "Could I also have ADHD?" or "Could I also be autistic?"
