One day you can run errands, cook and catch up with your sister. The next, getting out of bed feels like climbing a mountain. Flares and fatigue are some of the hardest parts of autoimmune disease, partly because they're so unpredictable.

## What a flare is

A flare is a period when your disease becomes more active. Symptoms get worse or new ones show up: more pain, swelling, rashes, fevers, brain fog or exhaustion. Flares can be triggered by stress, infections, too little sleep, sun exposure for some people, hormonal changes or stopping medicine. Sometimes there's no clear reason at all.

## Fatigue isn't regular tired

Autoimmune fatigue doesn't always get better with sleep. It can feel like your battery drains faster and recharges slower. Many people use "spoon theory," created by Christine Miserandino, who lives with lupus. You start each day with a limited number of spoons. Showering costs one, a work meeting costs three, a hard conversation costs four. When you're out, you're out.

## The push-and-crash cycle

On good days, it's tempting to catch up on everything. Then you crash for days. This boom-and-bust pattern is common, and it can make flares and fatigue worse. Pacing, which means doing a steady amount of activity and resting before you're empty, can help break the cycle.

> A lot of us were raised to believe rest has to be earned. "I'll rest when the work is done." But with an autoimmune disease, the work is never done, and pushing through can cost you days. Rest is part of your treatment.

## Try this

- **Learn your early signs.** Many people notice small changes before a full flare, like more joint stiffness, poor sleep or feeling irritable.
- **Make a flare plan.** Decide ahead of time who to call, what to cancel and what helps. See [Make a flare plan](#skill-flare-plan).
- **Pace your days.** Try [Pace, don't push](#skill-pacing), even on good days.
- **Protect your sleep.** A regular bedtime and wake time can help your body recover.
- **Tell your doctor about fatigue.** It can come from the disease, low iron, thyroid problems, depression or medicines. Some of these are treatable.
