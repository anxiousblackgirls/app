You say no, and then you keep talking. You explain your schedule, apologize twice, offer a backup plan, and promise to make it up to them. By the end, the no barely counts. For many anxious women, over-explaining is a way to manage the fear of disappointing someone.

## Why we over-explain

Anxiety treats someone else's disappointment like danger. A long explanation feels like protection: if they understand every reason, maybe they won't be upset with you. Over time, it trains your brain to believe a no has to be earned.

> Many Black women carry an extra worry: that a plain no will get them labeled difficult, cold, or angry. That worry has roots. One workplace study found that observers were more likely to blame a Black woman's anger on her personality than on the situation, and rated her as a weaker performer and leader. Another study found women of both races were penalized for anger compared with men. Either way, many of us learned to cushion every boundary so it couldn't be read as attitude. Research on Black women has also linked self-silencing, holding back your needs to keep the peace, with more symptoms of depression.

## Assertiveness can be learned

Assertiveness means standing up for your own interests while respecting other people's. A review of decades of research found that assertiveness training lowered anxiety, including social anxiety, and reduced symptoms of depression. It is a skill you practice, not a personality you either have or don't.

## Try this

- **Use the short version.** "No, I don't have the space for that." Then stop talking. Let the silence sit. It feels long to you and normal to everyone else.
- **Try "I don't."** In studies on sticking to goals, people who told themselves "I don't" ("I don't take calls after 7") held to their choices better than people who said "I can't." "I don't" sounds like a decision, because it is one.
- **Hold back the backup plan.** You don't owe a substitute, a referral, or a promise to help next time.
- **Notice the urge to explain.** When you feel it, take one breath before you speak. Ask yourself whether the extra words are for them or for your anxiety.
- **Start with low-stakes nos.** Practice on a store survey or a vendor email before the family group chat.
- **Expect discomfort.** Guilt after a boundary usually means you did something new, not something wrong.
