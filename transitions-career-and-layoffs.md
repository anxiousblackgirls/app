Losing a job, leaving corporate, or starting a business can shake your finances and your sense of self. In 2025, many Black women faced these changes all at once.

## What's happening

> Black women's unemployment rate reached 7.1 percent in late 2025, according to Bureau of Labor Statistics data reported by Ms. Magazine, more than double the rate for white women. Black women make up about 12 percent of the federal workforce, roughly twice their share of the overall labor force, so federal layoffs and cuts to diversity programs hit them especially hard. At the same time, research by American Express has found that Black women have been one of the fastest-growing groups of business owners in the US.

## The mental health side of a career change

- Shame, even when the layoff had nothing to do with your performance
- Fear about money, especially if others depend on you
- Losing your daily structure and coworkers
- Identity loss if your title was a big part of who you are
- Anxiety about starting over, or starting something of your own

## Through a Black lens

Many of us were taught that a good, stable job was the safest path. Losing one can feel like letting down the people who sacrificed for you. Starting a business can bring excitement and fear, especially without family wealth to fall back on.

## Try this

- **Separate the event from your worth.** Write: "I lost my job because ___." Then write: "I am still ___." List your skills, wins, and strengths.
- **Make a 30-day money plan.** List your essential bills, file for unemployment if eligible, and call creditors early to ask about hardship options.
- **Keep a routine.** Wake up at the same time, get dressed, and set two or three small goals each day.
- **Tap your network.** Tell five people what kind of work you're looking for. Many jobs come through people, not postings.
- **Talk to someone** if job loss is turning into hopelessness, heavy drinking, or trouble getting out of bed.
