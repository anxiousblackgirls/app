Month after month of hoping and then grieving. Appointments, shots, and bills. Watching friends announce pregnancies while you smile through it. Infertility affects your body, and it can wear down your mind.

## The stress is serious

In a well-known study, researchers compared the stress levels of women with infertility to those of women with cancer, HIV, and heart disease. Women with infertility had levels of anxiety and depression similar to women facing those other serious diagnoses. That tells you something important: if infertility has you struggling, your reaction fits what you're going through.

Common feelings include:

- Grief every time a cycle doesn't work.
- Jealousy or sadness around pregnant friends and baby showers.
- Anger at your body, God, or the unfairness of it.
- Worry about money and whether treatment will work.
- Feeling like you're losing yourself to the process.

> Many Black women carry infertility alongside the expectation to be strong and keep going. In one study of Black women with infertility, many said they felt like less of a woman, and nearly all kept their pain mostly to themselves. When you add the pressure to look fine at work, at church, and with family, the stress has no outlet. Grieving, asking for help, and taking breaks from trying are all ways of caring for yourself.

## Try this

- **Name the losses.** Each negative test or failed cycle is a loss. Give yourself time to feel it before jumping to the next plan.
- **Set limits on fertility talk and research.** Pick times to read, track, and plan, then step away.
- **Skip events when you need to.** You can send a gift and sit out a baby shower.
- **Keep one thing that's just yours.** A hobby, a class, or a weekly outing that has nothing to do with trying to conceive.
- **Ask your clinic about counseling.** Many fertility clinics offer mental health support, and therapists who specialize in infertility can help.
- **Watch for depression.** If you feel hopeless, stop enjoying things, or can't function for two weeks or more, talk to a professional.

If you're having thoughts of hurting yourself, call 911 or call or text 988.
