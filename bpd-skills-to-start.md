Dialectical behavior therapy (DBT) is the most studied treatment for borderline personality disorder. A full program happens with a trained therapist, often with a weekly skills group. These are a few of its ideas you can start practicing now.

## STOP

When emotions spike and you want to react:

- **S**top. Freeze for a moment.
- **T**ake a step back. Breathe.
- **O**bserve. Notice what you feel and what is happening.
- **P**roceed mindfully. Choose what to do based on what you want long term.

## Check the facts

Strong emotions tell a story. Ask yourself what actually happened, what you are assuming, and whether your reaction fits the facts. A late text back usually means someone is busy, not that they are leaving.

## Wise mind

DBT describes an emotion mind and a reasonable mind. Wise mind is where they meet. Before a big decision, pause and ask, "What does my wise mind know about this?"

## Opposite action

When an emotion pushes you toward something that won't help, try the opposite. If shame tells you to hide, reach out to one safe person. If anger tells you to attack, step away and speak gently later.

## Slow breathing

Breathe in for four counts and out for six. Longer exhales tell your body it is safe. Do it for two minutes.

> Finding DBT that feels culturally safe can take work. When you search for a therapist, ask if they offer DBT and if they have experience with Black women. Some programs offer DBT skills groups online.

## Try this

Pick one skill from this article and practice it once a day for a week, even when you're calm. Skills work best in a crisis when they're already familiar.

If urges to harm yourself feel strong, call 911 or call/text 988 now. You don't have to wait until it gets worse.
