The body you have at 25 isn't the body you'll have at 35, 45 or 60. Pregnancy, breastfeeding, stress, illness, medication and perimenopause all change how we look and feel. Sometimes the hardest part is grieving the body you used to have while learning to live in the one you have now.

## Bodies are supposed to change

Weight shifts during perimenopause, stretch marks after a baby, scars from surgery, hair thinning from stress or PCOS, a softer middle that won't go anywhere. These changes are common, and many are tied to hormones and life stages more than effort. Yet many women blame themselves, hide in photos or stop doing things they love until they "get their body back."

## Positive body image has room for bad days

Researchers who study positive body image describe it as respecting and appreciating your body, accepting the parts you don't love and being kind to yourself about how you look. It can sit right next to days when you don't like what you see. Some women find "body neutrality" easier: caring for your body whatever it looks like that day.

> A lot of us watched our mothers and grandmothers give everything to everybody, then criticize their own bodies in the mirror. You can do it differently. Taking care of a changing body, through rest, movement you enjoy, good food and seeing your doctor, is a way to show up for the woman you're becoming.

## Try this

- **Buy clothes that fit now.** Keeping a closet of "someday" sizes can make you feel bad every morning.
- **Get in the picture.** Your family will want photos of you as you are.
- **Name what your body has done.** Carried a child, survived an illness, worked double shifts, held people up.
- **Talk to your doctor about changes that worry you,** like sudden weight gain, hair loss or exhaustion. Some changes have treatable causes.
- **Grieve if you need to.** It's okay to miss your old body. Feeling the loss can make room for acceptance.
