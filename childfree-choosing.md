Some women know early that they don't want children. Others come to the decision slowly. Either way, choosing a life without kids is a real choice, and more people are making it.

## You're not as alone as it feels

A 2024 Pew Research Center survey found that 47 percent of U.S. adults under 50 without children say they're unlikely ever to have them, up from 37 percent in 2018. The top reason was simple: they just don't want to. Women were more likely than men to say this. Other common reasons included wanting to focus on careers or interests, worries about the state of the world, and the cost of raising a child.

A Michigan study found that more than one in four adults described themselves as childfree. Their life satisfaction was about the same as parents'. The idea that women without children end up unhappy doesn't hold up in the research.

> For many Black women, the choice comes with extra weight. Motherhood has long been treated as central to Black womanhood. Scholar Patricia Hill Collins wrote about how motherhood carries deep cultural meaning in Black communities, tied to survival, family, and community strength. So saying "I don't want kids" can feel like you're turning your back on something bigger than you. It can also bring questions from family, church, and friends that feel like judgment. Your life can still be full of family, care, and legacy without children of your own.

## The mental load of the choice

The decision itself may feel settled, while the reactions to it wear you down. Constant questions, unsolicited opinions, and feeling left out as friends have babies can bring up loneliness, guilt, or self-doubt. Those feelings don't mean you made the wrong choice. They mean you're living against the grain.

## Try this

- **Know your reasons for yourself.** Write them down. You don't owe anyone an explanation, but knowing your why helps when doubts creep in.
- **Find your people.** Look for friends, online groups, or communities of childfree women, including Black women, who understand.
- **Prepare a short answer.** "Kids aren't part of my plan" is enough. You decide how much more to share.
- **Make room for mixed feelings.** You can be sure of your choice and still feel a pang at a baby shower.
- **Talk to a therapist** if pressure from others is affecting your mood or relationships.
