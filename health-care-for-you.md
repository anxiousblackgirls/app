Your doctor gave you a list: lose weight, cut salt, check your sugar, take these pills. It can feel like your health has turned into a set of numbers you keep failing. But taking care of yourself is about more than numbers. It's about how you live, rest and get support.

## Start with the basics

- **Sleep.** Poor sleep raises blood pressure and blood sugar. Protecting your sleep is part of your treatment.
- **Regular meals.** Skipping meals and then eating a lot late at night can make blood sugar swing.
- **Movement you enjoy.** It doesn't have to be the gym. Walk with a friend, dance in your kitchen, join a line dance class at church or stretch while you watch TV.
- **Medicine.** Take it as prescribed. If cost or side effects are a problem, tell your doctor or pharmacist. There are usually other options.
- **Appointments.** Keep them, and bring your questions.

## Food without shame

You don't have to give up your culture to take care of your health. Small changes add up: baked instead of fried some nights, more greens on the plate, a smaller portion of rice, water instead of soda. Ask for a dietitian who knows the foods you grew up with, whether that's Southern, Caribbean, West African or Latin American cooking.

## Care for your mind too

Stress, anxiety and depression make it harder to take care of your body. If you're feeling overwhelmed, numb or hopeless, getting help for your mental health can make everything else easier.

## Ask for help

Many of us are used to taking care of everyone else. Taking care of yourself might mean asking your partner to cook, your sister to drive you to an appointment or your kids to do more around the house.

> Our grandmothers grew gardens, walked everywhere and cooked from scratch, and many of them also carried stress no one talked about. You can borrow the best of what they did, like home-cooked food and community, and add what they didn't have: regular checkups, medicine that works and permission to rest.
