Scroll long enough and you'll see it: Black women mocked, sexualized, called angry, or attacked in the comments for simply existing. Scholar Moya Bailey named this specific hatred misogynoir, the meeting of racism and sexism aimed at Black women.

## What research shows

> Moya Bailey coined "misogynoir" and has written about how Black women face it in media and online, and how they push back by creating their own digital spaces and stories. Research on young people found that experiencing online racial discrimination was linked to more depression and anxiety, even after accounting for offline discrimination. Seeing traumatic race-related content online has also been linked to symptoms of depression and post-traumatic stress.

## How it can show up

- Racist or sexist comments under your posts
- Seeing Black women public figures torn apart online
- Memes that turn Black women into jokes or stereotypes
- Colorism and texturism in beauty content
- Feeling anxious, angry, or low after scrolling

## Protecting your peace online

You can stay connected without letting your feed wear you down. You get to decide what comes into your space.

## Try this

- **Curate your feed.** Mute or unfollow accounts that leave you feeling worse. Follow Black women who make you laugh, think, and feel seen.
- **Turn off comments or limit replies** on your own posts when you need to.
- **Don't read the comments** on stories about Black women in the news.
- **Block and report** harassment, and screenshot anything threatening in case you need it later.
- **Notice your body after scrolling.** If you feel tense or low, close the app and do something offline for 15 minutes.
- **Create, don't just consume.** Sharing your own voice, art, or joy is a way of taking space back.
