Family is often where boundaries matter most and feel hardest. In many Black families, support flows in every direction: money, childcare, rides, housing, advice. That closeness is a gift. It can also leave you drained if you're the one everyone leans on.

## What research shows

> A national study comparing racial and ethnic groups found that Black Americans gave and received more support from extended family and church members than many other groups, including help with money, rides, and childcare. Those networks are a source of strength. They can also mean a few people, often women, carry much of the giving.

## Signs you need a boundary with family

- You're the family bank, and you're struggling financially yourself
- You dread holidays or phone calls
- Relatives comment on your weight, hair, relationship status, or choices
- You're always the one who hosts, plans, and cleans up
- You feel guilty for having a life of your own

## Through a Black lens

"Family first" and "don't forget where you came from" are lessons many of us hold close. Setting limits can feel like betraying them. You can love your family deeply and still decide what you can give.

## Try this

Pick one situation and try one of these scripts:

- **Money:** "I love you, and I'm not able to lend money right now."
- **Comments:** "I'm not going to talk about my weight. How's your garden doing?"
- **Time:** "I can come for dinner, but I'll head out by 7."
- **Hosting:** "I'm not hosting this year. Who wants to take it?"
- **Big decisions:** "I've thought about it, and I've made my choice. I'm not asking for advice on this one."

Then:

- **Say it once, calmly.** You don't need to debate.
- **Follow through.** If you said you'd leave at 7, leave at 7.
- **Expect pushback** at first. People adjust when boundaries stay steady.
