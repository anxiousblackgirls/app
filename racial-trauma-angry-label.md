You push back on an unfair decision, calmly, with facts. Later, you hear you came across as "aggressive" or "intimidating." A white coworker said almost the same thing and was called "passionate." Many Black women learn to shrink their voices at work to avoid being labeled the angry Black woman.

## What the research shows

In a 2022 study, researchers showed people videos and recordings of employees reacting with frustration at work. The employees were Black women, white women, Black men and white men. When a Black woman showed anger, people were more likely to blame it on her personality instead of the situation. Because of that, they rated her as a weaker performer and a less capable leader. The researchers noted that Black women aren't actually angrier than anyone else. The stereotype does the damage.

## The cost of always managing your tone

Constantly editing yourself is tiring. You may smile through things that upset you, rehearse emails five times or stay quiet when something's wrong. Holding feelings in doesn't make them go away. It can show up as headaches, tight shoulders, trouble sleeping and snapping at home because work never felt safe.

> The "angry Black woman" image has been used for generations to dismiss Black women who speak up, from the workplace to the doctor's office to TV. Afro-Latinas often hear a version of it too: "too loud," "too much." Your feelings are valid even when you choose not to show them at work. Having somewhere safe to let them out matters.

## Try this

- **Name the impact, not just the feeling.** "This change puts our deadline at risk, and I'd like to talk through options." Facts give people less room to label you.
- **Ask questions that put the work on them.** "Help me understand how this decision was made."
- **Choose your moments.** You don't owe every comment a response. Ask yourself, "Is this worth my energy today?"
- **Let it out somewhere safe.** Vent to a friend, journal, move your body or talk to a therapist so the feelings don't build up.
- **Find allies who'll speak up.** A trusted coworker can say, "I didn't hear that as aggressive at all."
- **Set boundaries.** See [Boundaries at work](#article-boundaries-work).
