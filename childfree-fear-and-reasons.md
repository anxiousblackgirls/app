For some Black women, deciding not to have children is about wanting a different life. For others, fear is part of the decision: fear of dying in childbirth, of raising a Black child in this world, or of not being able to afford it. Those reasons deserve respect too.

## The fears have a basis

In 2024, Black women in the U.S. died from pregnancy-related causes at about three times the rate of white women, according to the CDC. In the national Giving Voice to Mothers study, women of color reported more mistreatment during pregnancy and birth than white women, including being ignored or having requests for help refused. If you've watched friends or family go through frightening births, or you've been dismissed by doctors yourself, your worry makes sense.

Money and the state of the world matter too. In a 2024 Pew survey of adults under 50 who don't expect to have children, 38 percent named worries about the state of the world as a major reason and 36 percent said they couldn't afford a child.

> Many Black women think about what it would mean to raise a Black child: the talks about police, the fight for fair treatment at school, and the worry every time they leave the house. Some decide that's not a weight they want to carry. Others want children badly and feel afraid at the same time. Your reasons are yours. Make the decision from a place where fear informs your choice without being the only voice in the room.

## Try this

- **Separate the fears.** Write down each fear and ask whether it's about your body, your safety, money, or the world. Some fears have solutions, and some don't.
- **Get information about your own health.** If pregnancy fears are part of this, a preconception visit with a doctor who listens can tell you more about your personal risks.
- **Consider all your paths.** Some women choose adoption, fostering, mentoring, or being a devoted auntie. Others choose none of these. All are valid.
- **Talk to a therapist about intense fear.** A strong fear of pregnancy or birth can be treated, whether or not you ever want children.
- **Check whether pressure is pushing you either way.** Ask yourself: if no one else had an opinion, what would I want?
