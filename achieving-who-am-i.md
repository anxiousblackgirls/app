When people describe you, they probably say strong, reliable, accomplished. But if you lost the title, stopped being the helper, or just sat still for a week, who would you be? For many Black women, that question is scary to ask.

## Strength as identity

> Researchers studying the Strong Black Woman role asked Black women to describe it. They named qualities like embodying and displaying strength, keeping emotions in, independence, caregiving, and staying resilient through hard times. Many women saw the role as a source of pride, and also said it left little room for their own needs or for being seen as fully human.

When strength becomes your whole identity, every struggle can feel like a threat to who you are. Asking for help can feel like losing yourself.

## How it shows up

- Not knowing what you like outside of work or caregiving
- Feeling lost on vacation or during unemployment
- Panic when you can't fix someone else's problem
- Being known as the "together" one and having nobody check on you
- Measuring your worth by how much you do for others

## You are more than your roles

Your worth doesn't come from being useful. You were worthy before you accomplished anything, and you'll be worthy if you never accomplish another thing. That can be hard to believe after years of earning your place.

## Try this

- **Write "I am" statements without roles.** Not "I am a mother, a manager, a leader." Instead: "I am someone who loves the ocean." "I am funny." "I am curious about plants."
- **Try something you're bad at,** just for fun. A class, a craft, a sport. Notice what it's like to do something only for joy.
- **Let someone take care of you,** even in a small way. Accept the help without paying it back right away.
- **Ask a close friend,** "What do you love about me that has nothing to do with what I do for you?"
