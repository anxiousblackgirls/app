Coming out is not one moment. It's a decision you make over and over: with family, at church, at work, at the cookout. In Black families, where closeness and loyalty run deep, the stakes can feel especially high.

## Why family acceptance matters

> The Trevor Project's 2020 national survey of Black LGBTQ young people found that only about one in three reported high family support. Those who did have high family support attempted suicide at nearly three times lower rates than those who didn't. Earlier research by Caitlin Ryan and colleagues found that LGBTQ young adults whose families accepted them had better self-esteem and health, and lower rates of depression and suicidal thoughts.

Family acceptance can be one of the most protective things in an LGBTQ+ person's life. It doesn't have to be perfect to matter.

## You set the pace

There's no right time or way to come out, and you never owe anyone the information. Safety comes first, especially if you depend on family for housing or money.

## Through a Black lens

Some Black families respond with silence: everyone knows, nobody says it, and your partner is introduced as your "friend." Others worry about how the family looks to the church or community. Some relatives come around slowly. Many Black LGBTQ+ women build chosen family, people who love them fully, alongside or in place of the family they were born into.

## Try this

- **Start with your safest person.** Choose the relative most likely to respond with love, and tell them first. Their support can help with others.
- **Plan for safety.** If you're worried about losing housing or support, have a backup plan and a friend you can stay with before you share.
- **Use a simple script:** "I'm telling you because I love you and I want you to really know me. I'm [lesbian, bi, queer, trans]."
- **Build chosen family.** Invest in the people who see all of you.
- **Under 25 and struggling?** The Trevor Project offers 24/7 support by text, chat, or phone at thetrevorproject.org.
