Your phone connects you to everyone: the group chat, the family thread, your boss, the news, and strangers in the comments. It can also mean you're never off duty and never fully at rest.

## What research shows

> In an experiment with college students, those who limited their social media use to about 30 minutes a day for three weeks reported less loneliness and depression than those who used it as usual. Research on young people has found that experiencing online racism and seeing traumatic race-related content online are linked to more depression and anxiety.

## Signs you need a digital boundary

- Checking your phone first thing in the morning and last thing at night
- Feeling anxious when you can't answer a message right away
- Comparing your life to people online
- Group chats that stress you out more than they support you
- Work messages at all hours
- Doom-scrolling news about violence against Black people

## Through a Black lens

Social media gives Black women community, joy, and a voice. It also exposes us to misogynoir, racist content, and constant bad news. And many of us are the person in the family group chat everyone expects to answer.

## Try this

- **Set phone-free times.** The first 30 minutes after waking and the last 30 before bed are a good start.
- **Mute, don't leave.** You can silence a group chat without leaving it. Check it once a day instead of all day.
- **Use a time limit.** Most phones let you set a daily limit for social apps. Try 30 to 60 minutes.
- **Turn off work notifications** after hours.
- **Curate your feed.** Unfollow accounts that make you feel worse and follow ones that make you feel seen.
- **Set a response norm:** "I check messages a couple of times a day, so I might not reply right away."
