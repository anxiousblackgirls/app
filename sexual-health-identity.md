Maybe you've always known. Maybe you're 34 and just realized your "best friend" feelings in college were something more. Questioning who you're attracted to, or how you want to love, can happen at any age, and it can bring up a lot when your family, church and community have strong opinions.

## More women are naming it

In 2024, Gallup found that 9.3 percent of U.S. adults identify as lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender or something other than heterosexual. More than half of them identify as bisexual. Women are more likely than men to identify as LGBTQ+, and among Gen Z women, nearly 1 in 3 do. Many women also describe attraction that shifts over time, or identities that don't fit neatly into one word.

## Exploration is allowed

You don't have to pick a label before you're ready, and you don't owe anyone a timeline. Exploring might look like reading, journaling, talking to queer friends, going to an LGBTQ+ event or noticing who you're drawn to without judging it. Your identity is real whether or not you've acted on it, and whether or not anyone knows.

Sexual health matters here too. Women who have sex with women can still get and pass STIs, including HPV, herpes and BV. Testing and Pap smears still apply. Use barriers, wash toys between partners and tell your provider who you're having sex with so you get the right care.

> In many Black families and churches, being queer gets treated as something foreign, "a white thing," or something to pray away. But Black LGBTQ+ people have always been part of our families and our movements. If you'd like to read more, see [Black, queer and whole](#article-lgbtq-black-queer-whole) and [Faith and sexuality](#article-lgbtq-faith-and-sexuality).

## Try this

- **Write it out.** "Who have I been drawn to? What did that feel like?" Nobody has to see it.
- **Find your people.** Look for Black LGBTQ+ groups online or in your city. Being around people like you can quiet a lot of fear.
- **Decide who gets to know, and when.** Coming out is your choice. Start with the safest person, not the most important one.
- **Plan for hard reactions.** Think through what you'll say if someone reacts badly, and who you'll call after.
- **Find an affirming provider** for both your mental and sexual health. You deserve care that doesn't make you edit yourself.
