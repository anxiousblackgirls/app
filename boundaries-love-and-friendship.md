In love and friendship, boundaries can feel like pushing people away. They actually do the opposite. They tell the people close to you how to love you well.

## Why we struggle

> Research on the Superwoman role found that many Black women resist being vulnerable and put others' needs first, even in close relationships. Women described being the one friends lean on, while rarely asking for support themselves. Dialectical behavior therapy (DBT) teaches specific skills for asking for what you need while keeping both the relationship and your self-respect intact.

## Signs you need a boundary

- You're always the listener and never the one listened to
- A partner checks your phone or controls who you see
- A friend only calls when they need something
- You agree to things in bed or in the relationship that you don't want
- You feel anxious before seeing someone you supposedly love

If a partner hurts you, threatens you, or controls your money, phone, or movement, that's abuse, not a boundary issue. Call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233 or text START to 88788.

## Healthy boundaries sound like

- "I need a night to myself this week."
- "I'm not okay with you going through my phone."
- "I can't talk right now, but I can call you tomorrow."
- "I want to hear about your day, and I need to share about mine too."

## Try this

Use the DBT skill DEAR MAN for one boundary this week:

- **Describe** the situation: "We've canceled plans three times."
- **Express** how you feel: "I feel hurt and unimportant."
- **Assert** what you need: "I need us to stick to plans or tell me early."
- **Reinforce** why it helps: "It'll help me feel closer to you."
- **Mindful:** stay on this one topic.
- **Appear** confident, even if you're nervous.
- **Negotiate:** "What would make that easier for you?"
