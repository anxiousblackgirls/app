If you've experienced sexual abuse or assault, sex and closeness can feel complicated long after. You might freeze, feel far away from your body, feel nothing, or feel panic from a smell or touch. These are common trauma responses, and healing is possible.

## You're far from alone

A CDC survey found that 21 percent of Black women in the U.S. have been raped in their lifetime, and 38 percent have experienced other forms of sexual violence, such as unwanted sexual contact or being pressured into sex. Many never told anyone. Some were children. Some were hurt by someone they loved or trusted.

## How trauma can show up in intimacy

- Feeling numb, checked out or like you're watching yourself from outside
- Going along with sex you don't want because saying no feels unsafe
- Panic, crying or anger during or after sex, sometimes without knowing why
- Avoiding sex or closeness altogether
- Using sex to feel wanted, then feeling empty after

None of this means you're broken. Your body learned to protect you, and it can learn safety again.

> Black women have been told to stay quiet about sexual harm to protect the family, the church or a Black man from the police. Some of us were told we "wanted it" because of how we looked. That silence protected the people who caused harm. You deserve to be believed and to heal on your own terms.

## Try this

- **Get support.** Call the National Sexual Assault Hotline at 800-656-HOPE. That's 800-656-4673. It's free, confidential and open 24/7.
- **Look for a trauma-informed therapist.** EMDR and trauma-focused therapy can help your body and mind process what happened.
- **Choose a stop word** with your partner, and agree that either of you can use it, no questions asked.
- **Ground yourself** if you start to drift: name five things you can see and feel the bed or floor under you.
- **Go at your pace.** Holding hands, cuddling or simply lying together are all intimacy. You decide what's okay today.
- **Set boundaries** about what you will and won't do. See [Boundaries in love and friendship](#article-boundaries-love-and-friendship).

If you're in danger right now, call 911. If you're thinking about hurting yourself, call or text 988.
