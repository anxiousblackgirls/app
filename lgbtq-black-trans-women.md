Black trans women have been at the front of movements for freedom, including the fight for LGBTQ+ rights. They also face some of the highest rates of discrimination and violence of any group in the country. Their mental health deserves care and protection.

## What research shows

> The 2015 U.S. Transgender Survey, the largest survey of trans people in the US, released a report on its Black respondents. Black trans people reported high rates of unemployment, poverty, homelessness, and mistreatment in health care, schools, and by police. Many had faced harassment or violence. The Trevor Project found that 59 percent of Black transgender and nonbinary youth had seriously considered suicide in the past year, and those with high family support attempted suicide far less often.

These numbers reflect how the world treats Black trans women, not anything wrong with who they are.

## What helps

- Being called by your name and pronouns
- Family and friends who stand by you
- Access to gender-affirming medical care
- Community with other trans people
- Safe housing and stable work

## If you're a Black trans woman

Your life matters, and your safety comes first. You deserve care from people who see you as you are. Look for clinics and therapists who are trans-affirming before you share personal information.

## If you love a Black trans woman

Your support can save a life. Use her name and pronouns, including when she's not around. Speak up when others disrespect her. Ask what she needs rather than guessing.

## Try this

- **Save Trans Lifeline:** 1-877-565-8860. It's a peer support hotline run by and for trans people.
- **Find affirming care.** The National Queer and Trans Therapists of Color Network (nqttcn.com) lists therapists, and local LGBTQ+ centers often know trans-affirming clinics.
- **Make a safety plan** for situations that feel risky: who knows where you are, who you'll call, and how you'll get home.
- **Connect with community,** like local or online groups for Black trans women.
