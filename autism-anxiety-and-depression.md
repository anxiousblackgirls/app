Many autistic adults also live with anxiety or depression. For Black women, these are often the first diagnoses given, sometimes for years before anyone considers autism.

## How common it is

> A review combining dozens of studies of autistic adults found that about one in four had a current anxiety disorder and about one in four had current depression. Over a lifetime, the rates were even higher: roughly four in ten for anxiety and more than one in three for depression.

These conditions often grow from the daily strain of masking, sensory overload, social exhaustion, and being misunderstood.

## When autism hides behind anxiety

Black girls and women are less likely to be identified as autistic. If you were treated for anxiety or depression but therapy never quite fit, autism may be part of the picture. Social anxiety in particular can overlap with autism, since both can involve stress around social situations. The difference is often in the cause: fear of judgment, or the effort of decoding social rules that don't come naturally.

## Therapy that fits

Standard therapy can still help autistic people, but it works better when adjusted. Autism-informed therapists may use more direct language, written summaries, visual tools, and a focus on changing your environment, not only your thoughts.

## Try this

- **Ask your therapist directly:** "Have you considered whether I might be autistic?" or "Do you have experience working with autistic adults?"
- **Request adjustments** that help you: written notes after sessions, clear agendas, video sessions from home, or a dim room.
- **Track what drains you.** For one week, note when anxiety spikes and what was happening. Sensory overload, social demands, and unexpected changes are common triggers.
- **Find community.** Many autistic people say meeting others like them eased their depression more than anything else.
