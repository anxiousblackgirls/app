Divorce or the end of a long relationship can feel like losing your future, your home, your friend group, and parts of yourself all at once. It's one of the most stressful life changes there is, even when it's the right choice.

## What research shows

> A large review combining dozens of studies found that divorce and separation were linked to higher risk of early death and poorer health, especially in the years right after. The researchers noted that most people recover well, and that the stress of the transition, not divorce itself, drives much of the risk. Support, routine, and caring for your health during the transition make a difference.

## What it can feel like

- Grief, even if you wanted to leave
- Relief mixed with guilt
- Anger at your ex, or at yourself
- Fear about money, housing, or raising children alone
- Shame or worry about what people at church or in your family will say
- Loneliness at night and on holidays

## Through a Black lens

Many Black women were raised to keep the family together at all costs. Church communities may pressure you to stay. Family may see divorce as failure. You may worry about becoming another statistic about Black families. Your peace and safety matter. Leaving a marriage that hurts you is not failure.

If you're leaving an abusive relationship, call the National Domestic Violence Hotline at 1-800-799-7233 or text START to 88788. Leaving can be the most dangerous time, and they can help you plan.

## Try this

- **Grieve it as a loss,** even if it was your choice. Name what you're missing: the person, the plans, the family picture.
- **Build a support team:** one friend for crying, one for practical help, and a lawyer or financial advisor if you need one.
- **Create new rituals,** especially for holidays and weekends without your kids.
- **Rediscover yourself.** Write down things you loved before the relationship, and try one again this month.
- **Consider a therapist** to help you work through the grief and rebuild.
