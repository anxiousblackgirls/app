For many Black women, the church is family, history, and home. For Black LGBTQ+ women, it can also be the place where they first learned that who they are is wrong. Holding faith and sexuality together can be painful, and it's possible.

## What research shows

> A study of Black lesbian, gay, and bisexual young adults looked at how religious faith and internalized homonegativity, negative beliefs about being gay that people absorb from the world around them, related to resilience. Faith itself was linked to more resilience. Internalized negative beliefs worked against it. Faith wasn't the problem. The shame some religious messages create was. The Trevor Project's survey of Black LGBTQ youth also found that most who had been through conversion therapy said a religious leader was involved, and those young people had higher rates of suicide attempts.

## You don't have to choose

Some Black LGBTQ+ women stay in their home church. Some find affirming churches. Some build a spiritual life outside of church walls. Some step away from religion. All of these are valid paths.

## Signs a message is harming you

- Feeling you have to pray your identity away
- Believing God loves everyone except you
- Shame that follows you even outside of church
- Avoiding relationships because they feel sinful

## Try this

- **Separate God from the message.** Write down what you were taught about your identity, and next to it, what you believe about God's love. Notice where they don't match.
- **Look for affirming faith communities.** Some denominations and many individual churches openly welcome LGBTQ+ members. Search for "affirming church" plus your city.
- **Read affirming voices.** Black LGBTQ+ ministers and theologians have written about faith and sexuality from a place of love.
- **Find a therapist who respects both.** You can ask, "Can you support me with both my faith and my sexuality?"
