Many women with PCOS spend years being told their symptoms are stress, weight, or nothing at all. If you've left appointments feeling unheard, the problem may be the care you received, not you.

## Long waits are common

In a survey of more than 1,300 women with PCOS, one in three waited over two years for a diagnosis, and almost half saw three or more health professionals before getting one. Only about one in three were satisfied with how they were diagnosed, and fewer than one in six were happy with the information they got. Women who waited longer and saw more providers were less satisfied, which makes sense. Every dismissal costs you time, money, and trust.

> For Black women, being dismissed in health care has a long history. A 2016 study found that half of the white medical students and residents surveyed believed at least one false idea about biological differences between Black and white people, such as Black people having thicker skin. Those who held these beliefs rated a Black patient's pain as lower. So when your concerns about periods, pain, hair, or weight get brushed off, that experience is real and documented. It can also make you dread going back, which is understandable. You still deserve care, and you're allowed to look for a provider who takes you seriously.

## Try this

- **Bring a written summary.** List your symptoms, how long you've had them, and how they affect your life. Keep it to one page.
- **Ask for specific tests.** "I'd like to be evaluated for PCOS. Can we check my hormone levels and blood sugar?"
- **Ask about the guideline.** "Are you following the 2023 international PCOS guideline?" A good provider won't be offended.
- **Ask for notes in your chart.** If a test or referral is declined, ask that the request and the reason be documented.
- **Bring someone with you.** A friend or partner can take notes and speak up if you freeze.
- **Get a second opinion.** An endocrinologist or a gynecologist with PCOS experience may help more than a general visit.
- **Debrief afterward.** Write down how you felt. If an appointment left you shaken, talk it through with someone you trust or a therapist.
