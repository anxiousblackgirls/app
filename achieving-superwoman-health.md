The Superwoman role has several parts: showing strength, holding in emotions, resisting help, being driven to succeed, and taking care of everyone else. New research shows these parts don't all affect your health the same way.

## What research shows

> A study of more than 200 Black women in the San Francisco Bay Area looked at racial discrimination, the Superwoman Schema, and allostatic load, a measure of wear on the body from chronic stress. Women who felt an intense drive to succeed and a strong obligation to help others showed more harm to their bodies from discrimination. Some other parts of the role, like projecting strength, seemed to soften the effects. The researchers concluded that the root problem is discrimination itself, not Black women's ways of coping.

## What this means for you

You don't have to throw away everything about being strong. Some parts protect you. The parts to watch most closely are:

- **The drive to succeed no matter what,** especially pushing through when your body says stop
- **The obligation to help,** especially saying yes to every request at work, church, and home

Those are the habits most likely to turn stress into physical wear.

## Keeping what serves you

Strength, resilience, and confidence can stay. What can change is how much you carry and how often you put yourself last.

## Try this

- **Audit your yeses.** For one week, write down everything you agreed to do for someone else. Mark the ones you wanted to do and the ones you felt obligated to do.
- **Drop one obligation.** Pick one item from the "obligated" list and hand it back, delegate it, or decline next time.
- **Redefine success for this month.** Write one goal that's about your well-being, like sleeping seven hours four nights a week, and treat it as seriously as a work goal.
- **Watch your body's signals.** Headaches, jaw clenching, and poor sleep after a busy stretch mean the drive is costing you.
