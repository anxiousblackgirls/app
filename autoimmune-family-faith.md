"You're too young to be sick." "Just claim your healing." "You were fine at the cookout last week." When you live with a chronic illness, the people who love you don't always understand. And in many Black families, where being strong and showing up is expected, saying "I can't today" can feel almost impossible.

## The pressure to keep showing up

Many of us are the ones who host Thanksgiving, drive Grandma to her appointments, help with the kids and still go to work. An autoimmune disease doesn't erase those roles, but it changes how much you can carry. Family may not notice, because a lot of what you deal with is invisible.

## When faith and medicine meet

For many of us, faith is a deep source of comfort. Prayer, church family and believing in healing can help you cope. Some people may suggest that taking medicine or seeing a therapist means you don't have enough faith. You can trust God and take your medicine. Many people see doctors, treatment and support as part of how their prayers are answered.

## Dating, friendships and being understood

Explaining your illness over and over gets exhausting. Some friends will drift. Others will surprise you. When you're dating, it's up to you when and how much you share. People who are right for you will make room for your reality.

> In many of our families, illness was kept quiet. "Don't put our business in the street." That privacy often came from protecting each other. But silence can also leave you alone with something heavy. You get to decide who's safe to tell, and what you need from them.

## Try this

- **Pick one person to tell first.** Choose the person most likely to listen, not the one with the most opinions.
- **Give people a job.** "Can you bring a plate by Sunday?" is easier to say yes to than "Let me know if you need anything."
- **Use a short script.** "Some days I have more energy than others. If I cancel, it's my body, not you."
- **Set a boundary around advice.** "I appreciate you caring. I'm working with my doctor on this."
- **Find people who get it.** Support groups through the Lupus Foundation of America, the National MS Society or the Foundation for Sarcoidosis Research can connect you with others, including Black women living with the same diagnosis.
- **Use the boundary script.** See [The boundary script](#skill-boundary-script).
