Some of us were high achievers before we could spell the word. Straight A's, the "mature" one, the kid who never needed help. As adults, that drive can look like success from the outside and feel like pressure on the inside. Knowing where it started can help you decide what to keep.

## High achieving vs. high pressure

Wanting to do well is healthy. It turns into a problem when your worth depends on performing, when rest feels lazy, and when a small mistake ruins your whole week. Researchers call that side of it perfectionism. A large review found perfectionism is linked to anxiety, depression and eating disorders. Another study of more than 40,000 young people found perfectionism has risen steadily since 1989, especially the pressure people feel from others to be perfect.

## Where it often starts

- **Love that felt earned.** Research on parents' "conditional regard" found that when children feel more loved after achieving and less loved after falling short, they work hard, but they also feel more shame, anxiety and pressure. Many parents did this out of love and fear.
- **Being the responsible one.** Oldest daughters and girls who helped raise siblings or took care of a parent often learned early that being capable kept the family steady.
- **Praise for being "grown."** Being called mature and low-maintenance felt good. It also taught some of us that needing help was a problem.

> Black girls often get these messages with extra weight. Many Black parents teach their children they have to be "twice as good," a lesson researchers describe as preparing kids for bias they will face. It came from real protection. A Georgetown Law study also found that adults see Black girls as needing less nurturing and protection than white girls, starting as young as age 5. Put those together, and many Black girls grow up believing they have to be exceptional to be safe, and that they have to manage on their own. In adulthood, that can show up as overworking, struggling to delegate, and feeling anxious the moment you slow down.

## Try this

- **Trace one rule back.** Pick a rule you live by, like "never ask for help." Ask yourself who taught it to you and what it protected you from then.
- **Separate effort from worth.** Finish this sentence: "Even when I don't achieve anything today, I am..." Write three endings.
- **Try "good enough" on purpose.** Choose one low-stakes task this week and do it at 80 percent. Notice what actually happens.
- **Rest before you've earned it.** Schedule a break before the work is finished.
- **Get support.** If the drive has turned into constant anxiety or burnout, a therapist can help you keep your ambition without the fear underneath it.
