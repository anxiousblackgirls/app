If sex hurts, a lot of women grit their teeth and push through. Maybe you think it's normal, that it's all in your head, or that bringing it up will hurt your partner's feelings. Pain during sex is common, it has causes, and many of them can be treated.

## You're not the only one

A large British survey found that about 7.5 percent of sexually active women had painful sex that lasted three months or longer. Many of them also felt anxious about sex, had less interest in it and found it hard to talk about. Pain and anxiety feed each other: when you expect it to hurt, your muscles tense, and tense muscles hurt more.

## Some causes to ask about

- **Fibroids.** These noncancerous growths in the uterus are very common for us. One study estimated that by age 50, more than 80 percent of Black women and nearly 70 percent of white women had developed them. Depending on where they grow, fibroids can cause deep pain during sex, heavy periods and pressure.
- **Infections and BV.** Yeast infections, BV and some STIs can cause burning or soreness.
- **Dryness.** Stress, birth control, breastfeeding and perimenopause can all lower natural lubrication.
- **Muscle tightness.** Sometimes the pelvic floor muscles clench on their own, a condition called vaginismus. Pelvic floor physical therapy can help.
- **Endometriosis, scarring or past trauma.** These deserve a real workup.

> Many of us were taught to tolerate pain, from "it's just cramps" to doctors who don't take Black women's pain seriously. Painful sex is a medical concern, and you deserve a provider who looks into it. For more on getting heard, see [Fibroids, heavy periods and exhaustion](#article-peri-fibroids-and-fatigue) and [Being believed in health care](#article-racial-trauma-health-care).

## Try this

- **Stop when it hurts.** Pushing through teaches your body to brace. You can pause, switch things up or stop.
- **Use plenty of lubricant.** Water-based or silicone-based lubricants are safe with condoms.
- **Track it.** Note where it hurts (at the opening or deep inside), when it started and what makes it better or worse.
- **Tell your provider directly:** "Sex has been painful for three months. I'd like to find out why."
- **Ask about a pelvic floor physical therapist** if your muscles feel tight or you can't tolerate exams.
- **Talk with your partner** outside the bedroom: "This hurts sometimes, and I'm getting it checked. Can we slow down for now?"
