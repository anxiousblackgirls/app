"When are you going to give me grandbabies?" "You'll change your mind." "Children are a blessing from God." If you've chosen not to have kids, you've probably heard all of these, sometimes at the same dinner table.

## The judgment is real

A 2017 study found that people rated women and men who chose not to have children as less fulfilled than parents. That judgment was driven by moral outrage, feelings of anger and disapproval, as if not having children were wrong. The researcher concluded that many people see parenthood as a moral duty, not just a life choice.

A Michigan study found something similar. People who had or wanted children felt much less warmth toward childfree adults.

> In many Black families and churches, children are seen as a gift, a duty, and proof of a woman's worth. Many of us heard "be fruitful and multiply" in church, and grew up watching women gain respect when they became mothers. So when you say you don't want kids, people may hear rejection of family, faith, or culture. Some may worry about who will carry on the family name, or about you being alone. Their reactions often come from love and fear. That doesn't make the comments easier to hear, and it doesn't make them your responsibility to fix.

## How it can affect you

Hearing the same questions again and again can make you anxious before family events, cause you to pull away from people you love, or make you question yourself. Some women feel they have to keep proving they're happy. That kind of pressure wears on your mental health over time.

## Try this

- **Pick your response ahead of time.** Light: "I'm the fun auntie, that's my role." Firm: "I've made my decision, and I'd like us to stop discussing it."
- **Decide who gets the full story.** You can share your reasons with people who are safe, and give everyone else the short version.
- **Talk to one ally in the family.** Having someone who backs you up can change how gatherings feel.
- **Separate faith from pressure.** Many people of faith live full lives without children. If this is weighing on you spiritually, talk with a faith leader you trust or a therapist who respects your beliefs.
- **Limit time with people who won't let it go.** You can love someone and still leave early.
