The group chat that used to buzz all day goes quiet. A friend from college feels like a stranger. You realize the people you text most aren't the ones you thought they'd be. Friendships change as adults, and that change can stir up a lot of anxiety.

## Friendship is shrinking for a lot of people

In 1990, 3 percent of Americans said they had no close friends. By 2021, that number was 12 percent, according to the Survey Center on American Life. The share with 10 or more close friends dropped from 33 percent to 13 percent. Work, moves, marriage, kids and caregiving all take time away from friendships. If your circle got smaller, you're not doing adulthood wrong.

It also takes real time to build a friendship. A University of Kansas study found it takes about 50 hours together to go from acquaintance to casual friend, about 90 hours to become friends, and more than 200 hours to become close friends.

## There are levels, and that's okay

Researcher Robin Dunbar found that most people's relationships fall into layers: about 5 people in your closest circle, about 15 good friends, and larger circles of friends and familiar faces beyond that. Not everyone is meant to be in your top five.

- **Inner circle:** the people you call when it's bad news at 2 a.m.
- **Good friends:** people you trust and enjoy, even if you don't talk every week.
- **Season friends:** the work friend, the gym friend, the mom friend from your kid's school. Real, and sometimes temporary.
- **Lifelong friends:** people you can go months without talking to and pick right back up.

> For many Black women, friendship has been where we finally get to put the mask down. We spend so much energy code-switching at work and being strong at home that a friend who knows the real us can feel like oxygen. That's why being chosen for being yourself matters so much. A friend who likes the version of you that's tired, unsure, loud or quiet is protecting your mental health. And it's why a friendship fading can hurt like a breakup.

## Try this

- **Sort your circle without judging it.** Write down who's in each layer right now. Notice who has moved, and let that be information, not failure.
- **Invest in the few.** Put your limited time into the people who leave you feeling like yourself.
- **Let some friendships be seasonal.** You can be grateful for a friend and still let the closeness change.
- **Make the first move.** Adults often wait to be invited. Send the text, suggest the walk, start the standing date.
- **Grieve the ones that ended.** Losing a close friend is a real loss. Talk about it the way you would any other heartbreak.
