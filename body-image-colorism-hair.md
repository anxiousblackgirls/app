"You're pretty for a dark-skinned girl." "Don't stay out in the sun too long." "Your hair is so much easier when it's pressed." Many of us heard comments like these before we were old enough to question them, often from people who loved us.

## Colorism is real, and it shows up in self-esteem

Colorism is the preference for lighter skin, looser curls and features closer to European standards. It started with slavery and still shapes who gets called beautiful, who gets hired and who gets cast as the love interest. In a classic study of Black Americans, sociologists Maxine Thompson and Verna Keith found that skin tone was linked to self-esteem for Black women, more than for Black men. Lighter-skinned women often face a different kind of pain: being told they aren't "Black enough," or that their looks are all people see.

## Hair is never "just hair"

Black women's hair has been policed at school, at work and at home. The 2023 CROWN Workplace Research Study surveyed nearly 3,000 working women. Black women's hair was about 2.5 times as likely to be seen as unprofessional. Two in three Black women had changed their hair for a job interview, and among women 25 to 34, more than 1 in 5 had been sent home from work because of their hair. Many states have now passed the CROWN Act to ban hair discrimination, but the pressure doesn't disappear with a law.

> For a lot of us, the first lessons about beauty came from the kitchen chair: the hot comb, the relaxer, being told to sit still. Those were often acts of love from women trying to protect us in a world that punished natural hair and darker skin. You can honor that love and still unlearn the message that your natural self needs fixing.

## Try this

- **Trace the message.** Write down the first thing you remember being told about your skin or hair. Who said it, and what were they afraid of?
- **Change the compliments you give.** Skip "pretty for a dark-skinned girl" and "good hair." Just say "You're beautiful."
- **Wear your hair the way that feels like you,** whether that's natural, braids, a silk press or a wig. The goal is choice.
- **Know your rights.** If your state has a CROWN Act, hair discrimination at work or school may be illegal.
- **Protect the little ones.** Tell the girls in your life, often and specifically, that their skin and hair are beautiful.
