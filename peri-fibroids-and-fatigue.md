Heavy, flooding periods in your 40s aren't something you just have to live with. For many Black women, fibroids are part of the picture, and the blood loss behind them can quietly drain your energy and your mood.

## Fibroids and Black women

Fibroids are noncancerous growths in the uterus. They can cause heavy or long periods, pelvic pain or pressure, frequent urination, and bloating. Perimenopause can make bleeding even less predictable.

> A study using ultrasound screening found that by age 50, more than 80 percent of Black women had developed fibroids, compared with nearly 70 percent of white women. Black women also tended to develop them earlier and have larger, more numerous fibroids.

## The mental health connection

Heavy bleeding can lead to iron deficiency and anemia. Low iron causes exhaustion, brain fog, irritability, shortness of breath, and trouble concentrating, which can look a lot like depression. Some women are treated for low mood when part of the problem is low iron. Constant worry about bleeding through clothes at work or church also takes a real emotional toll.

## You have options

Treatments range from medication that lightens bleeding to procedures that shrink or remove fibroids, with hysterectomy as only one option among many. Ask about all of them, including ones that keep your uterus, if that matters to you.

## Try this

- **Track your bleeding.** Note how often you change pads or tampons and whether you pass large clots. Soaking through a pad or tampon every hour for several hours is a reason to call your provider.
- **Ask for blood work:** "Can you check my iron and ferritin levels, not just my hemoglobin?" Ferritin shows your iron stores.
- **Ask about every option:** "What treatments are there besides hysterectomy, and what are the pros and cons of each?"
- **Get a second opinion** if you feel rushed toward surgery or brushed off.
