Getting an autoimmune diagnosis can feel like the end of the life you planned. It doesn't have to be. Many Black women with lupus, MS, sarcoidosis, thyroid disease and other conditions build full lives: careers, relationships, families and joy. It takes some adjusting, and it takes support.

## Build your care team

You may need more than one doctor. A rheumatologist, neurologist, dermatologist, endocrinologist or kidney specialist may each play a part. A therapist who understands chronic illness can be just as important. If a provider doesn't listen or explain things clearly, you're allowed to find another one.

## Take your treatment seriously

Many autoimmune medicines take weeks or months to work, and some have side effects. It's common to want to stop when you feel better or when side effects are rough. Talk to your doctor before you stop, because stopping suddenly can trigger a flare. Ask about cost help if a medicine is too expensive.

## Care for your mind

Depression and anxiety are common with autoimmune disease. Getting help for them can make it easier to manage everything else, from taking medicine to getting through flares.

## Find your rhythm

- Learn your early warning signs and have a flare plan.
- Pace your activity so good days don't turn into crash days.
- Protect your sleep and eat in a way that gives you energy.
- Move your body in ways that feel good on that day: stretching, walking, swimming or dancing in your kitchen.
- Make room for joy that has nothing to do with your illness.

> Our grandmothers had a saying: "Do what you can, and leave the rest to God." Living well with a chronic illness isn't about doing everything. It's about knowing what matters most to you, doing that, and letting the rest go without guilt.

## Try this

- **Write down your care team.** List each provider, what they treat and how to reach them.
- **Keep a running question list.** Add to it between appointments.
- **Plan one joyful thing a week.** Put it on the calendar like an appointment.
- **Connect with others.** Look for support groups, online or in person, for your diagnosis.
