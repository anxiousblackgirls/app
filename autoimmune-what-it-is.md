Your joints ache for no reason. You're exhausted after a full night's sleep. A rash shows up and fades. Your hair is thinning, your hands swell, your thyroid is "a little off." For a lot of Black women, these are the first signs of an autoimmune disease, and they're easy to brush off as stress.

## What autoimmune disease is

Your immune system is supposed to protect you from germs. With an autoimmune disease, it gets confused and attacks your own body. Depending on the disease, it can go after your joints, skin, kidneys, thyroid, nerves, lungs or gut. There are dozens of these conditions, and most of them affect women far more than men.

## Some hit Black women harder

- **Lupus.** Black women are about three times as likely as white women to develop lupus. They tend to get it younger, often in their 20s and 30s, and are more likely to have serious complications like kidney disease.
- **Sarcoidosis.** This disease causes clusters of inflamed cells, often in the lungs, skin and eyes. In a national study, Black women had the highest rate of sarcoidosis of any group.
- **Multiple sclerosis (MS).** Doctors were taught for years that MS was a "white disease." A large Kaiser Permanente study found that Black women actually had a higher rate of new MS diagnoses than white women.
- **Thyroid disease, rheumatoid arthritis, scleroderma and others** also show up in our communities, often alongside each other.

## Why it can take so long to find out

Autoimmune diseases often come and go, and early symptoms can be vague: tiredness, aches, low fevers, brain fog. Tests aren't always clear at first. Many women see several doctors before getting answers, and some are told it's in their head. That delay is common, and it isn't your fault.

> Many of us grew up watching our mothers and aunties push through pain and exhaustion because there was no other choice. We learned to keep going. That strength is real, and it can also mean we ignore our bodies until something breaks. Paying attention to a symptom that won't go away is a way of taking care of yourself.

## Try this

- **Write down what you've noticed.** Note symptoms, when they started and what makes them better or worse.
- **Ask about family history.** Autoimmune diseases can run in families. Ask your relatives if anyone has lupus, thyroid disease, MS, sarcoidosis or "arthritis" at a young age.
- **Ask for a referral.** If your symptoms keep coming back, ask your doctor whether a rheumatologist should take a look.
- **Take care of your mind too.** Read [Autoimmune disease and your mental health](#article-autoimmune-mind-body).
