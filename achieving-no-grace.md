A coworker misses a deadline and it's "a crazy week." You make one typo and it comes up in your review. You can't remember the last time you got the benefit of the doubt. Many Black women feel they have no room to be human at work, and research backs that up.

## Held to a higher standard

In a well-known study, researchers Ashleigh Rosette and Robert Livingston asked people to evaluate leaders after their company had a good or bad year. When the company struggled, Black women leaders were judged more harshly than Black men or white women in the same situation. One bad quarter cost them more.

Other research shows the same pattern. Lean In found that 40 percent of Black women said they had to provide more evidence of their competence, compared with 30 percent of women overall. And in a survey of Black professionals, 65 percent said Black employees have to work harder to advance, compared with 16 percent of white professionals.

## What it does to your body and mind

Living with no margin for error keeps your nervous system on alert. You may triple-check every email, over-prepare for every meeting, say yes to everything and never take a sick day. That can look like excellence from the outside. On the inside it often feels like anxiety, exhaustion and resentment, and it can lead straight to burnout.

> "You have to be twice as good to get half as far." Our parents and grandparents said it to protect us, in Black American, Caribbean and African homes alike. It got many of us through doors. It can also leave you believing you're only as good as your last mistake. You deserve the same grace you give everyone else.

## Try this

- **Pick your "good enough" tasks.** Not everything needs 110 percent. Decide which work truly matters and let the rest be done well, not perfectly.
- **Own mistakes briefly.** "I missed that. Here's the fix." Skip the long apology. Then move on.
- **Track your wins.** Keep a running list of praise, results and saves. Bring it to reviews. See the Evidence file skill in the Pocketbook.
- **Give yourself the grace you'd give a coworker.** Ask, "What would I say to a colleague who made this mistake?"
- **Watch for burnout.** If you're running on empty, read [Burnout isn't a personal failure](#article-burnout-not-a-personal-failure).
