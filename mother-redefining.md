Many Black women are deciding what kind of mother they want to be, instead of repeating what they were handed. That can mean parenting with more softness, saying no to doing it all alone, or choosing motherhood on a timeline that works for them.

## Motherhood has always been bigger for us

Sociologist Patricia Hill Collins wrote that Black motherhood has never been limited to one woman raising her own children. Grandmothers, aunties, church mothers, and neighbors, whom she called "othermothers," shared the work of raising kids. Black mothers have also long been expected to work, protect their children from racism, and hold the family together, often all at once.

> That history gave us strength, and it also gave us pressure. Many of us watched our mothers sacrifice everything and never rest. Researcher Cheryl Woods-Giscombé found that Black women often feel obligated to show strength, hide emotions, and resist help, a pattern she called the Superwoman Schema, and that this can harm our health. Redefining motherhood can mean keeping the love and the fight while letting go of the idea that a good mother never breaks. You can admire what your mother did and still choose another way.

## What redefining can look like

In a 2025 study of 31 Black mothers, about two out of three said they disciplined their children differently from how they were raised, mostly with less spanking and yelling. Many said learning about child development changed their approach. Many also said their mothers did the best they could with what they had. Both things can be true.

Redefining motherhood can also mean:

- Going to therapy and telling your kids it's normal to get help.
- Talking about feelings at home, including your own.
- Asking for help without apology.
- Rebuilding a village on purpose, with friends, family, and paid support.

## Try this

- **Write your parenting values.** List three things you want your kids to feel at home, and one pattern you want to end.
- **Name one thing you're keeping.** What did your mother or grandmother do that you want to pass down?
- **Expect pushback.** Family may call gentler parenting "soft." You can say, "I'm doing what works for my child."
- **Give yourself the grace you give your kids.** You'll repeat old patterns sometimes. Repair counts.
