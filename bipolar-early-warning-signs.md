Bipolar episodes usually don't arrive out of nowhere. Most people have early warning signs days or weeks before a full episode. Learning yours is one of the strongest tools you have.

## Common early signs

> A review of studies on early signs of bipolar episodes found that most people could identify changes before an episode started. The most common early sign of mania was needing less sleep. Early signs of depression often included losing interest in things and pulling away from people.

**Before a high**, people often notice:

- Sleeping less without feeling tired
- More ideas, more plans, more talking
- Spending more, or feeling extra confident or irritable

**Before a low**, people often notice:

- Losing interest in friends and activities
- Sleeping much more
- Feeling slowed down or hopeless

## Why this matters for Black women

Many of us are praised for being busy, driven, and always on the go. Early mania can look like a "productive season," so family and coworkers may cheer it on. On the other side, early depression can be read as being tired or "in a mood." Knowing your own pattern helps you and the people close to you tell the difference.

## Try this

- **Write your personal warning signs.** Think back to past episodes. What changed first: sleep, spending, talking, appetite, church attendance, texting? List three signs for highs and three for lows.
- **Pick a trigger to act on.** For example: "If I sleep under five hours two nights in a row, I call my psychiatrist."
- **Share the list with one trusted person** and tell them what to say if they notice a sign.
- **Track sleep every day.** It is the earliest and most reliable sign for many people.
