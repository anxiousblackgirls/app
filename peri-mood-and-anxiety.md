If anxiety or sadness showed up out of nowhere in your 40s, or an old struggle came roaring back, perimenopause may be part of the reason. The hormone swings of this stage affect mood as much as they affect your periods.

## What research shows

> The SWAN study followed women for more than a decade. Women were more likely to have an episode of major depression during perimenopause and in the early years after menopause than before it, even after accounting for stress and past depression. A separate SWAN analysis found that women with low anxiety before midlife were more likely to develop high anxiety during and after the transition.

## How it can look

- Rage that comes fast and surprises you
- Crying more easily, or feeling flat and numb
- Racing thoughts at night
- Panic attacks for the first time
- Losing patience with people you love
- Feeling like you're "losing your mind" because your focus slips

## Through a Black lens

Midlife for many Black women is already full. We're raising kids, caring for aging parents, carrying leadership at work, and holding up church and community. When mood changes hit, it's easy to blame the load and keep pushing. Mood symptoms can also get labeled as attitude, especially for Black women, instead of being recognized as a medical change. You deserve to have your symptoms taken seriously.

## Treatment options

Therapy helps, and so can medication. For some women, hormone therapy eases mood symptoms along with hot flashes and sleep problems. Antidepressants and other options also work for many. These decisions belong with a provider who knows your full health history.

## Try this

- **Track mood next to your cycle** for two months. Patterns tied to your period point to hormones.
- **Ask directly:** "Could my mood changes be related to perimenopause? What are my options, including hormone therapy?"
- **Tell one person what's going on.** "I'm in a hormonal season and I'm not myself some days" helps the people close to you respond with patience instead of hurt.

If you're having thoughts of suicide, call or text 988 right now.
