Finding a therapist who understands Black women is hard enough. Finding one who also understands Black LGBTQ+ women can feel impossible. It's worth the search, because affirming care and community are some of the strongest protections there are.

## Community protects

> Psychologist Ilan Meyer's minority stress research found that connection to LGBTQ+ community can buffer the effects of prejudice on mental health. Research with Black lesbians found that in the face of racism, sexism, and heterosexism, women drew strength from community, spirituality, and pride in who they are. The Trevor Project found that Black LGBTQ youth with at least one supportive person in their lives attempted suicide less often.

## What affirming care looks like

- Your provider uses your name, pronouns, and the words you use for your relationships
- You don't have to educate them about basic LGBTQ+ or Black experiences
- Your sexuality or gender isn't treated as the cause of every problem
- Your partner is welcomed if you want them involved
- You feel safe being honest

## Questions to ask a potential therapist

- "What's your experience working with Black LGBTQ+ women?"
- "How do you understand the effects of racism and homophobia on mental health?"
- "Do you have training in LGBTQ+ affirming care?"

A good therapist will welcome these questions.

## Try this

- **Search directories built for you:** the National Queer and Trans Therapists of Color Network (nqttcn.com) and Therapy for Black Girls (therapyforblackgirls.com), which lets you filter by specialty.
- **Find one community space.** Look for Black LGBTQ+ events, Pride events organized by Black communities, book clubs, or online groups.
- **Name your supportive people.** Write down two or three people who love all of you, and reach out to one of them this week.
- **Celebrate yourself.** Mark the days that matter to you, like Black Pride or the anniversary of coming out to yourself.
