Many Black women say they spent years bringing perimenopause symptoms to doctors and leaving with nothing but "it's stress" or "lose some weight." Getting real care in this season often takes preparation and persistence.

## Weathering and midlife

> Public health researcher Arline Geronimus coined the term "weathering" to describe how constant stress from racism and hardship wears down Black women's bodies early. Her research found that Black women showed higher signs of biological stress (allostatic load) than white women, with the gap widening through middle age. Midlife health concerns like high blood pressure, sleep problems, and mood changes often arrive on top of that wear.

That means perimenopause for Black women can overlap with other health issues, and every symptom deserves attention.

## What treatment can look like

- **Hormone therapy.** The Menopause Society's current guidance states that for most healthy women under 60, or within 10 years of their last period, the benefits of hormone therapy for hot flashes and related symptoms generally outweigh the risks. Your personal and family history matter, so this is a conversation, not a default.
- **Non-hormone medications** for hot flashes, sleep, and mood.
- **Therapy** for anxiety, depression, and the life changes of midlife.
- **Checking your heart and bones,** since risks for both change after menopause.

## Try this

- **Bring a one-page summary** to your appointment: your top three symptoms, how long you've had them, and how they affect work, sleep, and relationships.
- **Use direct language:** "I believe I'm in perimenopause and these symptoms are affecting my life. I'd like to discuss treatment, including hormone therapy."
- **If you're dismissed,** ask, "What would need to happen for you to consider treatment?" and request that your concerns be noted in your chart.
- **Find a specialist.** Search for a Menopause Society Certified Practitioner at menopause.org.
