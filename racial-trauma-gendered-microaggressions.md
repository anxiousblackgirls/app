"Can I touch your hair?" "You're so articulate." "Why are you so angry?" "You're pretty for a dark-skinned girl." Black women hear comments like these because we're both Black and women. Researchers call them gendered racial microaggressions.

## What research shows

> Psychologists Jioni Lewis and Helen Neville developed a scale to measure gendered racial microaggressions against Black women. They identified four types: assumptions of beauty and sexual objectification, being silenced and marginalized, the Strong Black Woman stereotype, and the Angry Black Woman stereotype. Later studies using the scale linked these experiences to more psychological distress and depression.

## Common examples

- Comments or touching related to your hair
- Being seen as aggressive when you're assertive
- Being sexualized or called "exotic"
- Being talked over or ignored in meetings
- Assumptions that you can handle anything, so you get no support
- Colorism: being treated differently based on how dark your skin is

## Why "micro" doesn't mean small

Each comment might seem minor to the person saying it. For you, it's one more in a long line, and the stress adds up. Over time, it can lead to anxiety, anger, and exhaustion.

## Try this

- **Choose your response in advance.** You don't owe anyone a lesson, but it helps to have options ready:
    - Ask a question: "What do you mean by that?"
    - State the impact: "That comment felt dismissive."
    - Set a limit: "Please don't touch my hair."
    - Let it go and process it later with someone you trust.
- **Debrief after.** Text a friend, write it down, or say it out loud. Holding it in keeps it alive in your body.
- **Know your rights.** Hair discrimination at work and school is illegal in many states under laws like the CROWN Act.
- **Find your mirror.** Spend time with Black women who affirm your beauty, voice, and worth.
