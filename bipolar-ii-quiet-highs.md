Bipolar II is easy to miss. Its highs, called hypomania, are milder than full mania. They can feel like your best self: energized, confident, getting everything done. The lows are what usually bring people to a doctor, so many are diagnosed with depression instead.

## What hypomania can look like

- Needing only a few hours of sleep and feeling fine
- Taking on several new projects at once
- Talking faster and feeling unusually social
- Being more irritable or impatient than usual
- Spending more, or making quick big decisions

It lasts at least four days and is a clear change from how you usually are. Other people often notice it before you do.

> Research on bipolar disorder in Black Americans has found that it is often misdiagnosed and undertreated, and that Black patients are underrepresented in bipolar research. A Black woman with bipolar II may be treated for depression for years, or have her high periods seen as attitude or ambition.

## Why the difference matters

Depression alone and bipolar II are treated differently. Antidepressants without a mood stabilizer can sometimes trigger hypomania or mania, or make mood swings more frequent. Getting the right diagnosis protects you.

## Try this

- **Take a screening questionnaire.** The Mood Disorder Questionnaire (MDQ) is a short, widely used screening tool for bipolar disorder. Search "Mood Disorder Questionnaire PDF," fill it out, and bring it to your provider. It is not a diagnosis, but it starts the right conversation.
- **Ask someone close to you** whether they've noticed periods when you were very different: more energetic, less sleep, more spending.
- **Tell your provider about the highs,** even if they felt good. Say, "I've had times when I barely slept and felt great. Could this be bipolar II?"
