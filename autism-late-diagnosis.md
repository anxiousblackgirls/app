Many Black autistic women go years without knowing they are autistic. Some find out as adults. Some only realize it when their own child is diagnosed.

## What autism is

Autism is a difference in how the brain processes the world, especially social communication and sensory input. It is not an illness and it does not look one way. In women it can include:

- Studying people closely to figure out how to act
- Feeling drained after social events, even fun ones
- Strong reactions to sounds, lights, textures, or smells
- Needing routines and feeling thrown off by sudden changes
- Deep, focused interests
- Taking words literally or missing hints others catch

> A study of Black children with autism found they were diagnosed about three years after their parents first raised concerns. Many were first given other diagnoses, such as behavior or learning problems. Earlier research found Black children were less likely than white children to have autism recorded in their records at all. For Black girls, the gap is often wider.

## Why it matters to know

A diagnosis is not a box. For many women it is relief. It explains years of feeling different, working twice as hard socially, and burning out. It also opens the door to support that fits how your brain works.

## If you think you might be autistic

Adult autism evaluations are done by psychologists and some psychiatrists. Ask if they have experience with adult women and with Black clients. Many autistic people also find self-understanding and community through autistic-led groups before or after a formal diagnosis.

## Try this

- **Write your sensory and social list.** Note sounds, textures, or situations that drain you, and the routines or interests that calm you. This list helps a clinician see the full picture.
- **Ask family about you as a child.** Questions like "Was I bothered by noise?" or "Did I play alone a lot?" can bring back details you've forgotten.
- **Read first-person stories** by autistic Black women. Seeing yourself in someone else's experience can help you decide whether to seek an evaluation.
