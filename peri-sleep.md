Waking up soaked at 3 a.m., kicking off the covers, then lying there with your mind racing. Sleep problems are one of the most common and most draining parts of perimenopause, and poor sleep makes anxiety, depression, and brain fog worse.

## What research shows

> SWAN researchers found that trouble sleeping became more common as women moved through the menopause transition, and that hot flashes and night sweats were linked to more sleep problems. Separately, a large national study found that Black workers were more likely than white workers to get less than seven hours of sleep a night across most types of jobs. Many Black women enter perimenopause already short on sleep.

## Why it matters

Sleep is when your brain resets your mood. A few weeks of broken sleep can make you more irritable, more anxious, and less able to cope. It can also look like depression or ADHD getting worse.

## Through a Black lens

Many of us are the last one to bed and the first one up, getting everyone else settled before we rest. Add night sweats and a mind that won't stop, and sleep becomes the first thing sacrificed. Protecting your sleep in this season is a health decision, not a luxury.

## Treatment options

Hormone therapy can reduce night sweats for many women. Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is a proven treatment for sleep problems and works in midlife too. Some non-hormone medications also help with hot flashes.

## Try this

- **Keep the room cool.** Use a fan, lighter bedding, and breathable sleepwear. Keep a glass of cold water by the bed.
- **Keep the same wake time** every day, even after a bad night.
- **Get out of bed if you can't sleep** after about 20 minutes. Read something calm in dim light, then go back when you're sleepy.
- **Ask your provider about CBT-I** and about treating night sweats directly.
- **Ask for help at home** so you aren't always the last one awake.
