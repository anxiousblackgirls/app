A high blood pressure reading. A climbing A1C. Headaches that won't quit, a racing heart, sleep that never feels like enough. Sometimes a diagnosis isn't only about your body. It can be your body's way of saying, "Something has to change."

## Your body keeps track

Researchers use the word "weathering" to describe how years of stress, including racism, money worries and caregiving, wear down the body faster. In one well-known study, Black women showed signs of this wear and tear at younger ages than white women, even when they had similar incomes. Another line of research, on "John Henryism," found that working extremely hard to overcome every obstacle can raise the risk of high blood pressure, especially when you don't have enough support.

## Questions worth asking yourself

A diagnosis can be a chance to look honestly at your life:

- **What's been weighing on me the most?** Work, family, money, a relationship, grief?
- **How am I really taking care of myself?** Am I sleeping, eating regular meals, moving my body and going to my appointments?
- **Who takes care of me?** Is there anyone I can lean on, or am I always the one holding everyone up?
- **What am I holding in?** Anger, sadness and fear don't disappear when we ignore them. They often show up in our bodies.

## What it might be signaling for your mind

Ongoing stress, anxiety and depression can all show up in the body as high blood pressure, trouble sleeping, cravings, headaches, stomach problems and tiredness. If you've been feeling overwhelmed, numb, irritable or hopeless, your diagnosis might be a sign to care for your mental health too. Therapy, rest and support are part of treating your body.

> Many of us were taught that strong Black women don't complain, don't stop and don't ask for help. That message helped our families survive. But your body is asking you to listen. Taking care of yourself isn't selfish. It's how you'll be here, and well, for the people you love.
