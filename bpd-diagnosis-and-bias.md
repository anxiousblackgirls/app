BPD is one of the most stigmatized diagnoses in mental health. For Black women, race and gender stereotypes can make getting an accurate diagnosis, and fair treatment, even harder.

## Stigma among providers

> A review on BPD and stigma found that some clinicians see people with BPD as manipulative or difficult to treat, and that these attitudes can lead to less empathy and worse care. The authors argued that this stigma is not supported by the evidence, since BPD is treatable and many people recover.

## Race and the diagnosis

A systematic review of personality disorders across racial and ethnic groups in the UK found that Black patients were recorded as having personality disorders, including BPD, at lower rates than white patients. The authors raised the possibility that clinicians miss personality disorders in Black patients, or diagnose something else instead, such as a psychotic disorder.

For Black women, there are risks in both directions. Real BPD can be dismissed as attitude or "drama." Reasonable anger at racism or mistreatment can be pathologized. An accurate diagnosis needs a provider who understands your history and your context.

## Try this

Advocate for yourself with these questions:

- "What symptoms led you to this diagnosis?"
- "How did you consider my history and experiences with racism or trauma?"
- "What other diagnoses did you consider?"
- "What treatment do you recommend, and do you offer DBT or know who does?"

Also:

- **Ask for your records.** You have a right to see your mental health records under federal law, with limited exceptions.
- **Get a second opinion** if a diagnosis doesn't fit or a provider treats you with disrespect.
- **Remember the label isn't you.** A diagnosis is a tool for getting the right care.
