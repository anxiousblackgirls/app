Black women have been sold two stories about sex. One says we're "too much," hypersexual and fast. The other says a good woman doesn't want sex at all, she just keeps the peace. Neither story leaves room for you to know what you actually like.

## The Jezebel myth

Sociologist Patricia Hill Collins calls these stories "controlling images." The Jezebel image, a Black woman who is always seductive and always available, goes back to slavery, when it was used to excuse the rape of enslaved women. It still shows up today. In a 2018 study using eye-tracking, people looked longer at the sexual body parts of Black women than white women, and a second set of experiments found Black women were more easily linked with objects and animals in people's quick, automatic associations.

Growing up under that gaze, a lot of us learn to shrink. We dress so nobody calls us "fast." We hold back in bed so a partner won't think we've "been around." Or we perform what we think a partner wants and lose track of what we want.

## Pleasure is part of health

Sexual health includes protection and testing. It also includes desire, comfort, consent and enjoyment. Knowing your own body helps you notice when something is wrong, ask for what feels good and say no to what doesn't. There's no right amount of sex to want. Wanting a lot, a little or none at all can all be healthy, as long as it's your choice.

> The church, the family and the culture often taught us that our bodies were something to guard or something to apologize for. You're allowed to see your body as yours: something to care for, enjoy and decide about. Claiming that takes back some of what was taken from the women who came before us.

## Try this

- **Catch the old script.** When guilt shows up during intimacy, ask, "Whose voice is that?" Name it, then come back to your body.
- **Get curious alone.** Learning what feels good to you, without a partner, is normal and healthy.
- **Practice one ask.** "Slower," "a little softer," "I like that." Short sentences are easier to say in the moment.
- **Check in after.** Ask yourself, "Did I feel present? Did I feel safe? Did I want that?"
- **Talk to someone** if shame, pain or past harm keeps getting in the way. A sex therapist or trauma-informed therapist can help.
