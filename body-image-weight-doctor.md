You go in for a sore throat, a rash or anxiety, and somehow the visit ends with "Have you thought about losing weight?" If you've started dreading the scale at the doctor's office, or putting off care altogether, you're not alone.

## Weight stigma affects your care

Weight stigma is negative judgment about people in larger bodies. Research shows it changes how patients are treated. A review of studies on weight bias in health care found that providers often spend less time with larger patients, assume they won't follow advice and blame symptoms on weight before looking for other causes. Patients who feel judged are more likely to delay or avoid care, which can lead to problems being caught later.

## BMI was built on white bodies

Body mass index (BMI) is a quick math formula based on height and weight. In 2023, the American Medical Association adopted a policy calling BMI an imperfect measure when used alone. The AMA noted that BMI cutoffs were developed mostly from data on earlier generations of white people, and that body shape and composition differ across racial and ethnic groups, sexes and ages. It recommended that doctors look at other measures alongside BMI, such as waist size, body composition and metabolic health.

> Black women get a double dose: biased assumptions about our bodies and a long history of not being believed about our pain. Being told "it's your weight" can feel like one more way of not being heard. You deserve care that looks at the whole picture of your health. See [Being believed in health care](#article-racial-trauma-health-care) for more.

## Try this

- **You can decline the scale.** Say, "I'd prefer not to be weighed today unless it's medically necessary." You can also ask them not to tell you the number.
- **Keep the focus on why you came.** "I understand weight can affect health. Today I'm here about my headaches. What else could be causing them?"
- **Ask the fairness question:** "What would you recommend for someone in a smaller body with these same symptoms?"
- **Ask for specific tests** like blood pressure, blood sugar and cholesterol, which tell you more than weight alone.
- **Bring someone with you** if past visits have left you shaken.
- **Find a weight-inclusive provider.** It's okay to switch doctors until you find one who treats you with respect.
