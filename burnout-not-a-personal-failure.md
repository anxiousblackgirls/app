Burnout is what happens when stress keeps coming and recovery never does. The World Health Organization describes it as a result of chronic workplace stress that hasn't been managed, with three parts: exhaustion, feeling cynical or detached from your work, and feeling less effective.

## Burnout or depression

> A review of research on burnout and depression found the two overlap heavily, especially in exhaustion and low mood. Burnout tends to center on work and ease when you step away. Depression follows you everywhere, including vacation. If rest doesn't help, or you feel hopeless in every part of life, talk to a provider about depression.

## Why Black women burn out

- **Doing more to be seen as equal.** Many of us were raised on "twice as good," and that pace doesn't stop.
- **Being the only one.** Lean In and McKinsey's Women in the Workplace research has found that Black women face more microaggressions and doubts about their competence than most other groups.
- **Invisible labor.** Mentoring other Black employees, leading diversity work, and being the one people come to with problems, usually without pay or credit.
- **Job insecurity.** Black women's unemployment rate rose to 7.1 percent in late 2025, more than double white women's, amid federal layoffs and cuts to diversity programs. Fear of losing your job keeps you overworking.

Burnout is a response to conditions, not proof you're weak or doing life wrong.

## Try this

- **Rate the three signs.** On a scale of 1 to 10, how exhausted, how detached, and how ineffective do you feel at work? Scores of 7 or higher in all three are a warning.
- **Find one drain to cut.** List your tasks and mark which ones are unpaid, invisible, or not in your job description. Pick one to hand back or stop.
- **Take real time off,** without checking email. If you can't take a week, take a full day.
- **Talk to someone** if exhaustion follows you outside of work.
