"It takes a village" sounds nice until you look around and there's no village. Maybe your family lives far away, your mom still works, or the aunties who raised you are getting older. Many Black mothers are raising kids with far less help than the generations before them.

## The village used to be built in

Patricia Hill Collins wrote about "othermothers," the grandmothers, aunties, neighbors, and church members who helped raise Black children. That shared care made it possible for Black women to work and still have kids looked after. Today, families are more spread out, grandparents often work longer, and many women move away for school or jobs.

Meanwhile, the load hasn't gotten lighter. An Institute for Women's Policy Research analysis found that about 81 percent of Black mothers were the main or co-breadwinner for their families. That's paid work on top of the unpaid work of raising kids and running a household.

> Many of us were raised to believe asking for help means we can't handle our business. So we don't ask, even when we're running on fumes. In a study of Black single mothers, not having enough money was the top reason they gave for feeling depressed, followed by stress and daily parenting hassles. Those are village problems. When no one shares the load, it lands on your mind and body.

## Try this

- **Build your village on purpose.** List the people who could help with one specific thing: school pickup, a meal, a listening ear. Ask one of them this week.
- **Trade with other parents.** Take turns with pickups, playdates, or a monthly kid swap so each parent gets a free night.
- **Look at your faith or community groups.** Many churches, mosques, and community centers have parent groups or child care help.
- **Pay for help where you can.** A sitter for two hours, a cleaning service once a month, or meal delivery on hard weeks is support, not luxury.
- **Find mothers like you.** Online or local groups for Black moms can replace some of what the old village gave: advice, laughter, and someone who gets it.
- **Lower the bar where it doesn't matter.** Takeout, unfolded laundry, and screen time on a hard day won't ruin your children.
