John Henry, the story goes, raced a steam drill to prove a man could outwork a machine. He won, and then he died. Researchers borrowed his name for a coping style many Black women will recognize: meeting every obstacle by working harder.

## What John Henryism is

> Epidemiologist Sherman James described John Henryism as high-effort coping: a strong belief that you can overcome any challenge through hard work and determination. His research found that among Black Americans with fewer resources, people high in John Henryism had higher blood pressure. Later reviews found the pattern is complex, but the core idea holds: pushing nonstop against barriers you didn't create has a cost.

## How it can look

- Believing every problem can be solved if you just try harder
- Feeling personally responsible when systems fail you
- Taking on more when you're already overwhelmed
- Feeling guilty when you rest
- Measuring your worth by your output

## Why it matters

Determination got many of us through school, careers, and family crises. It's a real strength. The problem comes when effort becomes the only tool, especially against barriers like racism and bias that no amount of hard work can fully overcome. Then the effort itself starts wearing you down.

## Try this

- **Separate what's yours.** Write down a current struggle. Draw two columns: "What I can control" and "What I can't." Pour your effort into the first column only.
- **Ask, "Is this a hard-work problem or a system problem?"** If it's a system problem, the answer might be support, advocacy, or walking away, not more effort.
- **Schedule a day of not achieving.** No goals, no productivity, no side hustle. Notice what comes up.
- **Talk about it.** Many Black women share this pattern. Saying it out loud with others who understand can loosen its grip.
